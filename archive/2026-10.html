Coasian Stakeholder Theory

科斯式利益相关者理论

Matthew J. Higgins, Peter G. Klein, Anita McGahan, Rob Wuebker

Abstract

Stakeholder theory asks who is in, who is out, and who gets what. Existing work illuminates important dimensions of firm–stakeholder relationships but does not supply a common, transaction-level rule connecting these questions. We use the Coase theorem to develop such a rule. A Coasian stakeholder is an actor with shutdown power, defined as the de facto capacity, within the relevant time horizon, to prevent, materially delay, or substantially reduce the expected joint value of a focal value-creating transaction. If all such actors are identified and bargaining and enforcement are costless, value-maximizing transactions proceed, although rights and bargaining positions may affect who captures value. When transaction costs are positive, however, identification, bargaining, coalition formation, and enforcement can delay, distort, or suppress those transactions. Across six propositions, we explain how shutdown power is identified and acquired, how complementarity and bargaining conditions shape value capture, how firms reshape stakeholder sets through coalition costs and governance design, and why consolidated opposition can sometimes preserve transactions that fragmented claims would suppress.

中文摘要

利益相关者理论追问谁在内、谁在外以及谁获得什么。现有研究揭示了企业—利益相关者关系的重要维度，但并未提供一条连接这些问题的、共同的交易层面规则。我们运用科斯定理来发展这样一条规则。科斯式利益相关者是拥有停摆权力的行为主体，停摆权力被定义为在相关时间范围内，阻止、实质性延迟或大幅降低某一焦点价值创造交易的预期联合价值的事实能力。如果所有此类行为主体都被识别出来，且讨价还价与执行均无成本，则价值最大化的交易会继续进行，尽管权利和讨价还价地位可能影响谁获取价值。然而，当交易成本为正时，识别、讨价还价、联盟形成和执行可能延迟、扭曲或抑制这些交易。通过六个命题，我们解释了停摆权力如何被识别和获取，互补性与讨价还价条件如何塑造价值获取，企业如何通过联盟成本和治理设计重塑利益相关者集合，以及为什么联合反对有时能够保全那些碎片化主张会抑制的交易。

Behavioral Finance at 40

行为金融学40年

Nicholas C. Barberis

Abstract

Behavioral finance seeks to improve our understanding of markets, investors, and firms by adopting a psychologically realistic description of how people think. While behavioral finance ideas have been part of human discourse for decades if not centuries, the modern research effort in the field began in the 1980s. Forty years later, behavioral finance is a vibrant area of financial economics. In this article, I explain why by reviewing some of its recent progress. I focus on three topics – beliefs about future earnings, beliefs about future returns, and prospect theory – and on their applications to understanding asset prices and investor behavior. I close by commenting on some new directions in the field – cognitive foundations, non-traditional decision processes, and artificial intelligence.

Who Can Control Our Firms? National Security and Foreign Ownership Regulation

谁能控制我们的企业？国家安全与外资所有权监管

Rodrigo Adão, Olivier Kooi

Abstract

Governments invoke national security to regulate foreign acquisitions of domestic firms. Why is ownership regulation a distinct policy margin beyond standard tools, like production subsidies and trade taxes? We show that contractual frictions make multinational firm boundaries central to geoeconomic policy. Within firms, ownership allocates residual control that affects outcomes through non-contractible decisions. Between governments, the absence of enforceable agreements makes the economic costs of conflict shape geopolitical bargaining. Foreign ownership depends on private returns to foreign control, but also determines national exposure to geopolitical conflict. It can increase foreign dependence, technology transfer, or sabotage scope. If such margins were contractible, the government could regulate them directly. When they are not, ownership regulation is the second-best tool that changes control rights and, thus, geopolitical outcomes. Consistent with our mechanism, U.S. regulation emphasizes the national-security risks of foreign control over firm decisions, has tightened with rising geopolitical tensions, and is tighter in sectors with higher exposure to defense demand and contractual frictions.

中文摘要

政府援引国家安全来监管外国对本国企业的收购。为什么所有权监管是超越生产补贴和贸易税等标准工具的一个独立政策边际？我们表明，契约摩擦使跨国企业边界成为地缘经济政策的核心。在企业内部，所有权分配剩余控制权，而剩余控制权通过不可契约化的决策影响结果。在政府之间，缺乏可执行的协议使冲突的经济成本塑造地缘政治讨价还价。外资所有权取决于外国控制的私人回报，但也决定国家对地缘政治冲突的暴露程度。它可能增加外国依赖、技术转移或破坏范围。如果这些边际是可契约化的，政府就可以直接监管它们。当它们不可契约化时，所有权监管就是改变控制权、从而改变地缘政治结果的次优工具。与我们的机制一致，美国监管强调外国控制对企业决策的国家安全风险，随着地缘政治紧张加剧而收紧，并且在国防需求暴露度和契约摩擦更高的部门更为严格。

Tariff Staggering

关税错峰调整

Stephanie Schmitt-Grohé, Martín Uribe

Abstract

Using a panel of U.S. imports and customs duties at the HS 6-digit level, we document that in a typical quarter about 20 percent of tariffs rise, 20 percent fall, and 60 percent remain unchanged. Motivated by these facts, we develop a model of staggered tariff setting and embed it in a dynamic open-economy framework. Staggering changes the dynamics of the cross-sectional mean tariff, generating hump-shaped macroeconomic responses to tariff shocks. Staggering also overturns the Razin–Svensson trade- balance neutrality result for permanent tariff increases, because the gradual adjustment of the mean tariff creates an anticipation effect that induces agents to front-load net imports. Inherited tariff dispersion has first-order macroeconomic effects, allowing tariff harmonization to be studied with linear approximation methods. We find that eliminating tariff dispersion while holding the mean tariff unchanged has effects akin to a transitory tariff cut in an economy without staggering.

Ratings, Debt, and Deficits: An Exploration

评级、债务与赤字：一项探索

Olivier J. Blanchard, Daniel Leigh, Prachi Mishra

Abstract

We look at the effects of debt and primary fiscal balances on sovereign credit ratings through the lens of a simple model. We find that the ratings differ from the implications of the model in three important ways. They give much more weight to debt relative to forecast primary balances. They understate the effects of the difference between the interest rate and the growth rate. They give a very large role to country effects. For the same level of debt and forecast primary balances, they imply extremely different ratings across countries, and imply extremely different levels of debt needed to reach a given rating.

The Impact of Silver Loading in ACA Health Insurance Marketplaces

ACA健康保险市场中的银级加载效应

Coleman Drake, David Anderson, Dylan Nagy, Mark Shepard

Abstract

In 2017, the federal government ended payments for cost-sharing reduction (CSR) subsidies for the Health Insurance Marketplaces created by the Affordable Care Act. Rather than uniformly increase Marketplace plans’ premiums to offset the loss of these payments, over 40 states responded by silver loading—only increasing the premiums of silver plans to which CSR subsidies apply and premium tax credits are tied. Silver loading was intended to increase the affordability of Marketplace coverage by increasing premium tax credits and thereby enrollment, though the literature lacks causal estimates. Furthermore, Congress is considering reinstating CSR subsidy funding, effectively undoing silver loading. We use panel data on 2015-2021 Marketplace health plan premiums and enrollment in 12 states from the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services to estimate silver loading’s effects using contiguous county-pair models. We find that silver loading functioned as intended, lowering the cost of Marketplace coverage and increasing enrollment by 8-14% among subsidized Marketplace enrollees with incomes between 250% and 400% of the poverty level. We project that Congress ending silver loading would decrease Marketplace enrollment by at least 330,000 enrollees in 2027.

When Cash Wakes Up: Inertia in Institutional Money Market Funds and the Potential Effects of AI Disruption

当现金苏醒：机构货币市场基金的惯性及AI颠覆的潜在影响

Ali Hortaçsu, Younggeun Yoo

Abstract

We study institutional cash management by constructing a novel dataset that records the specific money market funds (MMFs) held by each mutual fund in each month from 2010 to 2024. We document remarkably high inertia: more than half of mutual funds never switched their MMF choice throughout the sample period, despite non-negligible yield dispersion. To decompose the sources of this inertia, we develop a two-stage structural model that separately identifies the determinants of portfolio managers’ attentiveness (“waking up”) and their conditional preferences once attentive. We estimate the model using a methodology that combines BLP-style contraction mapping with maximum likelihood. We establish three main findings. First, MMFs with less attentive investor bases engage in greater liquidity transformation while offering lower yields. As a result, they earn higher profits and exhibit significantly higher survival rates, especially during periods of low interest rates. Second, we find that aggregate flows appear more yield-sensitive during low interest rate periods (the well-documented “reaching for yield” phenomenon) not because individual investors become more yield-seeking, but because a larger fraction of investors becomes attentive—a composition effect rather than a preference shift. Third, counterfactual analysis reveals a potential tension between market efficiency and financial stability if AI-driven automation reduces investor inattention. In the absence of inattention, fees fall by approximately half, yield dispersion collapses to near zero, and market concentration declines by roughly 20%, but monthly fund flows increase by about three times and the incidence of fund-months in which redemptions exceed a fund’s weekly liquid assets rises by about six times, raising the risk of fire sales and MMF runs.

Competition in Generative AI: Evidence from Consumer Demand

生成式AI中的竞争：来自消费者需求的证据

Mark L. Egan, Dominic Russel, Claire Shi, Vincent Yao

Abstract

How competitive will the market for generative AI be as it matures? Using novel transaction level credit and debit card data covering 174 million consumers and 114 billion transactions, we study the adoption of paid services and the demand-side foundations of competition in this market. As of April 2026, approximately 2.9% of U.S. consumers pay for at least one major generative AI service, with adoption highest among younger and higher-income consumers. Nearly all of these paid users subscribe to basic plans that cost $30 or less per month. Three patterns suggest limited differentiation and low switching costs. First, although multihoming is common, it has declined as providers’ capabilities have expanded. Second, annual customer retention rates range from 20% to 45% across major AI providers, lower than those for many other subscription products. Third, switching rises around major model releases. Motivated by these findings, we develop and estimate a structural model of AI service demand and supply that reveals strong competitive pressure. Consumers are highly price sensitive, have much weaker preferences for particular providers than for AI overall, and view AI services as increasingly close substitutes. Our results suggest that, absent a substantial increase in AI model quality differentiation, growing substitutability among services will constrain providers’ pricing power and allow consumers to capture a large share of the surplus generated by these services.

The Great Game: A Model of Geoeconomic Competition

大博弈：地缘经济竞争模型

Christopher Clayton, Matteo Maggiori, Jesse Schreger

Abstract

We build a model of two hegemons that have valuable trading relationships with each other and at the same time compete in exerting geoeconomic power over countries in the rest of the world. When the hegemons trade with each other, their optimal policy is shaped both by classic economic considerations - the profitability of that specific trade - and by geoeconomic competition - how the trade affects the power of each hegemon vis-a-vis the rest of the world. We show that containment, a policy mix in which a hegemon limits sales of its inputs to the rival hegemon and uses its power to demand that the rest of the world shift away from sourcing from the rival, arises when the two hegemons offer relatively substitutable exports, are less interdependent with each other, and have more direct geopolitical rivalry. Countries in the rest of the world benefit, all else equal, from hegemons containing each other since they partially squander their power with offsetting demands. However, containment policy can also worsen the efficiency of global production, leaving the rest of the world worse off.

中文摘要

我们构建了一个两个霸权的模型，它们彼此拥有有价值的贸易关系，同时在世界其他地区争夺施加地缘经济权力。当两个霸权相互贸易时，它们的最优政策既受经典经济考量——该特定贸易的盈利能力——的影响，也受地缘经济竞争——该贸易如何影响每个霸权相对于世界其他地区的权力——的影响。我们表明，当两个霸权提供的出口品相对可替代、彼此相互依赖程度较低、且具有更直接的地缘政治竞争时，就会出现遏制——一种政策组合，即一个霸权限制向对手霸权销售其投入品，并利用其权力要求世界其他地区减少从对手采购。在其他条件相同的情况下，世界其他地区的国家会从霸权相互遏制中受益，因为它们通过相互抵消的要求部分浪费了自身权力。然而，遏制政策也可能恶化全球生产效率，使世界其他地区处境更糟。

A Network Approach to Geopolitics

地缘政治的网络方法

Ernest Liu, Stephen J. Redding, Bennett Smith-Worthington, David Y. Yang

Abstract

We develop a tractable framework for analyzing geopolitics as a network of coordination and strategic influence. Countries choose geopolitical actions that trade off domestic interests against incentives to align with connected countries. They can exert costly bilateral influence to affect one another’s actions. We characterize equilibrium actions and bilateral influence in closed form. Cross-country differences in domestic interests decompose into latent geopolitical cleavages, each of which the network transmits into geopolitical actions independently of the others. The cross-country covariance matrices of domestic interests and of geopolitical actions identify the cleavages and their importance, from which we recover the network. We implement the framework using data on United Nations voting and democratic orientation. The inferred network is well approximated by a few dimensions, aligns with external measures of geopolitical ties, and recovers well-known historical cleavages. We undertake counterfactuals for domestic interests and network connections and quantify three notions of geopolitical power

Training Your Replacement: Worker Incentives and the Pace of Automation

培训你的替代者：工人激励与自动化速度

Joshua S. Gans, Peter Landry

Abstract

Firms ask workers to train AI systems that will replace them. We model this with a worker who can shirk, cooperate with training, or obstruct training while maintaining productivity. Because training ends the job but only cooperation completes training, dismissal becomes a weaker penalty for shirking. The firm must raise the worker’s rent, and the worker gains from their replacement. AI can create jobs paying more than they produce and eliminate jobs it never automates. Abstract

We study how firms choose among incentive contracts and how accurately managers predict their effects. Using a survey of managerial beliefs and a large-scale field experiment, we randomly assign agents at the market level to several widely used, expenditure-equivalent incentive schemes. In the field, the best-performing contract raises weekly firm profit by over 55% relative to the status quo, despite being ranked lower by managers, whereas the worst-performing contract performs as predicted. Managers reliably avoid poorly performing contracts but systematically underestimate top-performing ones. Standard contract theory does better as a forecaster here: an unbiased textbook principal orders the contracts correctly, or very nearly so, correctly identifying the profit leader where managers do not; where it does miss, the error is similar to managers’ and for a similar reason. We show that the performance differences across contracts reflect a mix of labor-supply and risk responses, and that managerial predictability varies with contract complexity and managerial hierarchy. Our results highlight the importance of contract design for firm performance and reveal systematic limits to managerial cognition in shaping incentives.
