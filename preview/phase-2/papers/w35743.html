Immigration and Child Mortality during the Age of Mass Migration

大规模移民时代的移民与儿童死亡率

Katherine Eriksson, Peter Zhixian Lin, Gregory Niemesh

健康与医疗 美国

Abstract

In the late nineteenth century, Americans feared that crowded immigrant neighborhoods were spreading disease through industrial cities. We study this claim using newly constructed town-level panel data from Massachusetts, 1860 to 1915, a setting uniquely suited to this exercise because its early vital registration system allows consistent measurement of mortality before national registration became widespread. Using a shift-share instrumental variable strategy based on historical settlement patterns, we estimate the effect of local exposure to immigration on mortality. The relevant question is not whether immigrant families themselves experienced higher mortality, but whether immigration altered the local disease environment more broadly. We find that increases in the foreign-born share significantly raised infant and child mortality, with the infant effect falling disproportionately on the post-neonatal period. Mechanical changes in the composition of births by parent nativity account for only a portion of the estimated effect, implying that the results are not driven primarily by higher mortality among immigrant births. Instead, the evidence is consistent with increased exposure to immigrants, communicable disease, and spillovers to children of native-born parents. These findings highlight immigration as one factor shaping the mortality transition in crowded high-mortality environments.

中文摘要

在19世纪末，美国人担心拥挤的移民社区正在通过工业城市传播疾病。我们使用新构建的马萨诸塞州1860年至1915年城镇层面面板数据来研究这一说法，这一背景特别适合此项研究，因为其早期生命登记系统允许在全国登记普及之前一致地测量死亡率。使用基于历史定居模式的移动份额工具变量策略，我们估计了当地移民暴露对死亡率的影响。相关问题不是移民家庭本身是否经历了更高的死亡率，而是移民是否更广泛地改变了当地的疾病环境。我们发现，外国出生人口比例的增加显著提高了婴儿和儿童死亡率，其中婴儿效应不成比例地落在新生儿后期。父母出生地构成的机械变化仅解释了估计效应的一部分，这意味着结果并非主要由移民出生婴儿的更高死亡率驱动。相反，证据与移民暴露增加、传染病以及向本土出生父母子女的溢出效应相一致。这些发现强调移民是塑造拥挤高死亡率环境中死亡率转变的一个因素。
