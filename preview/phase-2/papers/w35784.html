The Protective Effects of a Healthy Spouse: Medicare as the Family Member of Last Resort

健康配偶的保护效应：作为家庭成员最后依靠的医疗保险

Itzik Fadlon, Tal Gross, Alex Hoagland, Timothy Layton

健康与医疗 未指明区域

Abstract

We study the value of informal care from a spouse and how access to such care shapes optimal insurance coverage for long-term care. Using Medicare claims and a novel spousal crosswalk, we examine how the sudden incapacitation of a spouse affects their partner's use of formal care. A spouse's health shock increases their partner's probability of a nursing-home stay by 30%. We document significant negative health effects for the partner, but provide evidence that their increase in nursing-home stays is largely driven by substitution from informal care to formal care. When their partners are incapacitated, Medicare recipients become less sensitive to the price of formal care, consistent with a higher valuation of that care and reduced moral hazard. We develop a framework to interpret these findings. The framework translates differences in the level and price elasticity of demand for formal care into a lower-bound estimate of the value of informal care provided by a spouse. It also suggests that optimal insurance for long-term care should be designed at the household level with more generous coverage for those who do not have access to informal care.

中文摘要

我们研究配偶提供的非正式护理的价值，以及获得此类护理如何影响长期护理的最优保险覆盖。利用医疗保险索赔数据和一种新的配偶交叉匹配方法，我们考察配偶突然失能如何影响其伴侣对正式护理的使用。配偶的健康冲击使其伴侣入住养老院的概率增加30%。我们记录了伴侣显著的负面健康效应，但提供证据表明，其养老院入住增加在很大程度上是由非正式护理向正式护理的替代所驱动。当伴侣失能时，医疗保险受益人对正式护理价格的敏感度降低，这与对该护理更高估值和道德风险降低相一致。我们开发一个框架来解释这些发现。该框架将正式护理需求水平和价格弹性的差异转化为对配偶提供的非正式护理价值的下界估计。它还表明，长期护理的最优保险应在家庭层面设计，对无法获得非正式护理的人提供更慷慨的覆盖。
