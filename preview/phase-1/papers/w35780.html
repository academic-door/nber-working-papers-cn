Regulating Entrepreneurship: The Case of Capital Requirements

监管创业：资本要求的案例

Annika Bacher, Andreas Fagereng, Marius A. K. Ring, Ella Getz Wold

国际贸易 挪威

Abstract

Governments have long imposed minimum equity requirements on new corporations. Opponents view them as barriers to entry while proponents argue that they protect stakeholders from financially unviable businesses. We study this quantity-quality trade-off using a Norwegian reform and comprehensive data linking entrepreneurs to their firms. A 70% reduction in required capital nearly doubled entrepreneurial entry, with no deterioration in survival, profitability, productivity, or interest-bearing leverage and no differences in founders’ ex-ante income, liquidity, or ability. These results suggest capital requirements restrict entrepreneurship without screening on quality or liquidity. Rather, we highlight returns-to-scale heterogeneity as an important determinant of the observed entry response.

中文摘要

政府长期以来对新公司施加最低股权要求。反对者将其视为进入壁垒，而支持者则认为它们保护利益相关者免受财务上不可行的企业之害。我们利用挪威的一项改革以及将创业者与其公司联系起来的全面数据，研究这种数量与质量之间的权衡。所需资本减少70%几乎使创业进入翻倍，而存活率、盈利能力、生产率或有息杠杆没有恶化，创始人的事前收入、流动性或能力也没有差异。这些结果表明，资本要求限制了创业，却没有在质量或流动性上进行筛选。相反，我们强调规模收益异质性是所观察到的进入反应的重要决定因素。
