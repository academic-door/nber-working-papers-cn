Responses to Extreme Price Changes: Evidence from the Residential Demand for Natural Gas

对极端价格变化的反应：来自居民天然气需求的证据

Anna Alberini, Levan Bezhanishvili, Milan Ščasný, Roberton C. Williams III

其他主题 格鲁吉亚

Abstract

We examine the response of residential customers to an extreme price change – namely that imposed by the government in the Republic of Georgia during the pandemic lockdown in the winter of 2020 and early 2021. As of November 2020, the government paid the gas bill directly to the utility, effectively making gas free, as long as consumption was below or exactly 200 m3/month. The policy was lifted at the end of February 2021. We use quasi-experimental causal methods based on a control group comprised of permanent residents of an area along the North-South pipeline, who receive gas for free (up to the generous allowance of 700 m3/month) every winter, while the treatment group is comprised of households living in areas with similar climates and similar population density who received gas for free only during the pandemic free gas months. Controlling for the weather, the ATT of the policy is a 2-7% increase in gas consumption (in the treatment group compared to the control group). Back-of-the-envelope calculations suggest a price elasticity of demand of -6.5% to -11%.

中文摘要

我们考察居民客户对极端价格变化的反应——即格鲁吉亚共和国政府在2020年冬季和2021年初疫情封锁期间实施的价格变化。截至2020年11月，政府直接向公用事业公司支付燃气账单，只要消费量低于或恰好为200 m3/月，就实际上使燃气免费。该政策于2021年2月底取消。我们使用准实验因果方法，对照组由南北管道沿线地区的永久居民组成，他们每年冬季都免费获得燃气（最高可达700 m3/月的慷慨额度），而处理组由生活在气候相似、人口密度相似地区的家庭组成，这些家庭仅在疫情期间的免费燃气月份获得免费燃气。在控制天气后，该政策的ATT为燃气消费增加2-7%（处理组相对于对照组）。粗略计算表明，需求价格弹性为-6.5%至-11%。
