We've Got You Covered: Establishment-Level Evidence on California's Fast-Food Minimum Wage

我们为你提供保障：加州快餐最低工资的机构层面证据

Vitor C. Melo, David Neumark, Liam Sigaud, Gary A. Wagner

教育与劳动 未指明区域

Abstract

We study the employment effects of California’s $20 fast-food minimum wage. Evaluating this policy poses two challenges in commonly used aggregate datasets: coverage is based on 60-establishment chain-size threshold that cannot be identified; and there is measurement error in the industry codes used to identify fast-food restaurants. We address these challenges using establishment-level data. We find evidence of negative employment effects, but they are concentrated among small chains, among which the policy also slowed entry and increased exit. Thus, the primary effect of the fast-food minimum wage was to disadvantage small chains, albeit with modest overall employment effects thus far.

中文摘要

我们研究加州20美元快餐最低工资的就业效应。评估这一政策在常用总体数据集中面临两个挑战：覆盖范围基于60家机构连锁规模阈值，而该阈值无法识别；用于识别快餐店的行业代码存在测量误差。我们使用机构层面数据来应对这些挑战。我们发现负面就业效应的证据，但这些效应集中在小连锁店中，政策还减缓了小连锁店的进入并增加了退出。因此，快餐最低工资的主要效应是使小连锁店处于不利地位，尽管迄今为止总体就业效应较为温和。
