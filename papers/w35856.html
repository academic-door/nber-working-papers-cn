Which Workers Left the Labor Force Post-COVID Recession and Why?: Filing, Tax Credit Take-Up, and Labor Force Engagement by Occupation

新冠疫情衰退后哪些工人退出了劳动力市场，以及原因何在？：申报、税收抵免领取与按职业划分的劳动力市场参与

Andrew C. Barr, Laura Kawano, Bruce Sacerdote, Michael Stevens

金融与资产定价 未指明区域

Abstract

The COVID-19 pandemic caused a sharp initial rise in unemployment, followed by an unexpectedly tight labor market. Using a panel of tax data containing newly assembled information on worker occupation, we document persistent labor force exit, especially among low-income and low-skill workers. Low-skilled unmarried men are by far the most likely to exit, reinforcing recent concerns regarding the economic and social engagement of this group. Contrary to prevailing theories, we find little evidence that school closures, virus exposure, stimulus payments, or unemployment benefits explain these exits. Nor is the exit explained by additional upgrading of occupations or firm quality. Individuals who leave the labor force do not (on average) make up the missing income; however, self-employment income replaces roughly a quarter of the lost W-2 income. The facts suggest that the willingness to work for low pay decreased and preferences for leisure and job flexibility increased among a large set of workers who have found ways to get by with less income. These results have implications for tax policy, fiscal projections, and labor market dynamics in the post-pandemic economy.

中文摘要

COVID-19大流行导致失业率最初急剧上升，随后劳动力市场意外收紧。使用一个包含新汇编的工人职业信息的面板税务数据，我们记录了持续性的劳动力退出，尤其是在低收入和低技能工人中。低技能未婚男性迄今为止最有可能退出，这加剧了近期对该群体经济和社会参与的担忧。与流行理论相反，我们几乎没有发现证据表明学校关闭、病毒暴露、刺激支付或失业救济能够解释这些退出。退出也不能由职业或企业质量的进一步升级来解释。离开劳动力市场的个人（平均而言）并未弥补缺失的收入；然而，自雇收入替代了大约四分之一的损失W-2收入。这些事实表明，在一大批已找到以更少收入维持生计方式的工人中，为低薪工作的意愿下降，而对闲暇和工作灵活性的偏好上升。这些结果对税收政策、财政预测以及疫情后经济中的劳动力市场动态具有启示意义。
