Internalizing Externalities through Public Pressure: Transparency Regulation for Fracking and Water Quality

通过公众压力内部化外部性：针对水力压裂和水质的透明度监管

Pietro Bonetti, Christian Leuz, Giovanna Michelon

环境与气候 美国

Abstract

Responding to the rise of hydraulic fracturing (HF) and risks to water quality, U.S. states mandated disclosure for HF wells. We study whether and how such targeting of corporate externalities with transparency regulation reduces environmental impact. We examine salt concentrations considered HF signatures and find significant reductions in surface water impact between 9-17%, mostly along the intensive margin. Consistent with these findings, we document improvements in operator practices, including fewer wastewater spills. We unpack the transparency mechanism and show that transparency regulation enables social movements and increases public pressure, which in turn drives the documented changes in practices and water quality.

中文摘要

为应对水力压裂（HF）的兴起及其对水质构成的风险，美国各州强制要求对HF井进行信息披露。我们研究了这种针对企业外部性的透明度监管是否以及如何减少环境影响。我们考察了被视为HF特征标志的盐浓度，发现地表水影响显著降低了9%至17%，主要发生在集约边际上。与这些发现一致，我们记录了作业者实践的改进，包括废水泄漏的减少。我们解析了透明度机制，并表明透明度监管能够推动社会运动并增加公众压力，这反过来又推动了所记录的做法和水质变化。
