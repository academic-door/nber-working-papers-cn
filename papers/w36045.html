How AI Impacts the Quest for Knowledge: Evidence from AlphaFold2

AI如何影响知识探索：来自AlphaFold2的证据

Myra Mohnen, Joshua S. Gans

AI 与数字经济 未指明区域

Abstract

Scientists can respond to artificial intelligence by pushing the frontier of science and asking new questions, or by deepening their understanding of questions already studied. We study how AI shapes this choice in structural biology, where experiments examine a protein's three-dimensional structure and its function. AlphaFold2, released in 2021, predicts the structure of almost every known protein, with a confidence that varies across proteins. Linking 530,471 proteins to the experiments run on them between 2017 and 2025, we compare how experimental activity changed after 2021 across proteins with more and less confident predictions. Where predictions are more confident, the experimental record adds fewer first structures for proteins far from any relative of known structure. It adds more follow-up experiments on proteins whose structure is already known, and the rise is largest for proteins surrounded by relatives of known structure. A model of experimental choice shows how AlphaFold2 can deepen existing knowledge rather than extend it: a prediction replaces an experiment that would establish a protein's structure, and it lowers the cost of experiments that build on that structure.

中文摘要

科学家可以通过推进科学前沿并提出新问题来回应人工智能，也可以通过加深对已有研究问题的理解来回应。我们研究AI如何在结构生物学中塑造这一选择，在该领域中实验检验蛋白质的三维结构及其功能。AlphaFold2于2021年发布，可预测几乎所有已知蛋白质的结构，其置信度因蛋白质而异。我们将530,471种蛋白质与2017年至2025年间对其进行的实验相关联，比较2021年之后预测置信度较高和较低的蛋白质在实验活动上的变化。在预测置信度较高的地方，实验记录为那些与任何已知结构的近亲都相距甚远的蛋白质新增的首次结构较少。它为结构已知的蛋白质新增了更多后续实验，而且这一增长在周围存在已知结构近亲的蛋白质中最大。一个实验选择模型展示了AlphaFold2如何能够加深而非扩展已有知识：一项预测取代了本可确定蛋白质结构的实验，并降低了基于该结构开展实验的成本。
