The Geopolitical Trap: How Great Power Rivalry Protects Rising Powers

地缘政治陷阱：大国竞争如何保护崛起中的强国

Johann Caro-Burnett, Sebastian Galiani, Gustavo Torrens

其他主题 中国相关 德国、美国

Abstract

Why do rising powers survive periods in which established states can still suppress them? Existing theories of preventive war emphasize commitment problems between declining and rising states, which can make adverse power shifts especially dangerous. We show that commitment problems among established rivals can have the opposite effect. Preventive action against a rising power can alter the incumbent’s relationship with another state. Under opportunistic rivalry, prevention temporarily exposes the incumbent to exploitation by an existing rival. Under defensive rivalry, successful prevention strengthens the incumbent by eliminating a potential counterweight, giving its rival an incentive to protect the rising state. In both cases, third-party responses can deter preventive action that would occur in an otherwise identical dyadic setting. Cooperation among established powers can reverse this result when credible arrangements allow them to manage the strategic consequences of suppression. Historical evidence from the American Revolution, China’s nuclear emergence, and U.S.–Soviet bargaining over West German nuclear capabilities illustrates the mechanisms and their limits. The analysis shows how rivalry among established powers can protect vulnerable rising states and thereby facilitate changes in the international distribution of power.

中文摘要

为什么崛起中的强国能够在既有强国仍可压制它们的时期生存下来？既有的预防性战争理论强调衰落国家与崛起国家之间的承诺问题，这可能使不利的权力转移尤其危险。我们表明，既有对手之间的承诺问题可能产生相反效果。针对崛起强国的预防性行动可能改变 incumbent 与另一国家的关系。在机会主义竞争下，预防行动会暂时使 incumbent 暴露于既有对手的利用之下。在防御性竞争下，成功的预防行动会通过消除潜在制衡者而增强 incumbent，从而使其对手有动机保护崛起国家。在这两种情况下，第三方反应都可能阻止在原本相同的双边情境中会发生的预防性行动。当可信安排使既有强国能够管理压制带来的战略后果时，既有强国之间的合作可以逆转这一结果。来自美国独立战争、中国核崛起以及美苏围绕西德核能力谈判的历史证据，说明了这些机制及其局限。该分析表明，既有强国之间的竞争如何保护脆弱的崛起国家，从而促进国际权力分布的变化。
