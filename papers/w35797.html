Property Rights Uncertainty, Prices, and Speculation: Evidence from China's Housing Market

产权不确定性、价格与投机：来自中国住房市场的证据

Hanming Fang, Jing Wu, Vincent Yao

其他主题 中国相关

Abstract

We examine the causal effect of uncertainty in property rights on housing prices and speculative behavior. We take advantage of a distinctive setting in Shenzhen, China, where neighboring residential units that are otherwise similar differ in the strength of their property rights protections. Some units have full property rights (FPR) with a 70-year leasehold, whereas others have only limited property rights (LPR) protections. Using detailed listing data, we find that the sales market prices these protections, while the rental market does not. Our estimates imply that the perceived probability that FPR rights could be questioned at the end of the 70-year term is between 7% and 21%, and that the probability the LPR receives no legal protection in any future year exceeds 50%. We further show that LPR units are more susceptible to speculation, as reflected in higher turnover and greater price volatility. Lastly, we show that the public release of new urban planning codes raises listing prices and lowers turnover and price volatility for LPR properties relative to matched FPR units.

中文摘要

我们考察产权不确定性对房价和投机行为的因果效应。我们利用中国深圳的一个独特情境，在那里，其他方面相似的相邻住宅单元在产权保护强度上存在差异。一些单元拥有完整产权（FPR），附带70年租赁期，而另一些单元仅拥有有限产权（LPR）保护。使用详细的挂牌数据，我们发现销售市场对这些保护进行了定价，而租赁市场则没有。我们的估计意味着，FPR权利在70年期限结束时可能受到质疑的感知概率在7%至21%之间，而LPR在任何未来年份都得不到法律保护的概率超过50%。我们进一步表明，LPR单元更容易受到投机影响，这反映为更高的换手率和更大的价格波动。最后，我们表明，新城市规划法规的公开发布提高了LPR房产相对于匹配FPR单元的挂牌价格，并降低了换手率和价格波动。
