How Big is Small? The Economic Effects of Access to Small Business Government Support

小企业有多大？获得小企业政府支持的经济效应

J. David Brown, Matthew Denes, Ran Duchin, John Hackney

金融与资产定价 美国

Abstract

We study the effects of vast increases in U.S. small business program eligibility standards, which expanded larger firms' access to support for small businesses. Exploiting quasi-random variation in the timing of these expansions and using administrative Census data, we show that revenues decline for the smallest firms, particularly those that are younger, more productive, and financially constrained. Government procurement contracts also are reallocated to larger firms. Consequently, firm exits increase, wages decline, and patenting falls. These findings highlight the economic consequences of expanding eligibility: by crowding out the smallest firms, resources shift away from high-potential firms, reducing dynamism and innovation.

中文摘要

我们研究了美国小企业计划资格标准的大幅提高的影响，这些提高扩大了大企业获得小企业支持的渠道。利用这些扩张时间上的准随机变化，并使用行政人口普查数据，我们表明最小企业的收入下降，尤其是那些更年轻、生产率更高且财务受限的企业。政府采购合同也被重新分配给较大的企业。因此，企业退出增加，工资下降，专利申请减少。这些发现凸显了扩大资格的经济后果：通过挤出最小企业，资源从高潜力企业转移，从而降低了活力和创新。
