Crowding Out Temptation: Experimental Evidence on Savings and Health Behaviors in Kenya

挤出诱惑：肯尼亚储蓄与健康行为的实验证据

Harsha Thirumurthy, Elizabeth F. Bair, Dean Yang, Teniola I. Egbe, Benard Ayieko, Julius Wesonga, Sue Napierala, Connie Celum, Mary E. Putt, Carol S. Camlin, Mark Ayallo

健康与医疗 肯尼亚

Abstract

Alcohol use, transactional sex, and gambling carry significant health and economic consequences. We conducted a randomized controlled trial in Kenya to assess whether these behaviors could be altered with a savings intervention that provided lottery-based interest, goal-setting support, and savings reminders. Over 24 months, the intervention increased formal savings and assets, financed primarily by lower spending on alcohol, transactional sex, and gambling. It also reduced hazardous drinking by 26 percent and intimate partner violence by 15 percent. HIV/STI incidence declined by 8 percent, though this was not statistically significant. Savings interventions can simultaneously boost financial security and change health behavior.

中文摘要

饮酒、交易性行为和赌博会带来重大的健康和经济后果。我们在肯尼亚开展了一项随机对照试验，评估是否可以通过一种储蓄干预来改变这些行为，该干预提供基于抽奖的利息、目标设定支持和储蓄提醒。在24个月内，该干预增加了正规储蓄和资产，其资金来源主要是减少在饮酒、交易性行为和赌博上的支出。它还将有害饮酒减少了26%，将亲密伴侣暴力减少了15%。HIV/STI发病率下降了8%，尽管这一结果在统计上不显著。储蓄干预可以同时提升财务安全并改变健康行为。
