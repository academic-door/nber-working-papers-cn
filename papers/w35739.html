A Sin Ban, Not a Sin Tax? Consumption and Incidence of SNAP Soda Restrictions

禁令而非罪税？SNAP含糖饮料限制的消费与归宿

Crossan Cooper, Katja Seim, Jintaek Song

其他主题 未指明区域

Abstract

We study the first statewide SNAP restrictions on sweetened beverages and candy. Restricted spending falls 11 percent; calories and sugar fall 5–8 percent. Substitution is asymmetric: eligible fruit drinks partly offset soda declines, while candy restrictions reduce purchases of eligible snacks. Retailers barely change shelf prices. Losing SNAP’s sales-tax exemption raises tax-inclusive prices only 1.9 percent. Spending declines are larger at retailers with greater SNAP use and remain large online, where checkout stigma is less salient. Nationwide adoption would impose 13 percent of the out-of-pocket burden of an equivalent excise tax, while welfare gains reach roughly $1.1 billion annually.

中文摘要

我们研究了首个在全州范围内对含糖饮料和糖果实施的SNAP限制。受限支出下降11%；热量和糖分下降5–8%。替代效应是不对称的：符合条件的果汁饮料部分抵消了汽水消费的下降，而糖果限制则减少了符合条件零食的购买。零售商几乎不改变货架价格。失去SNAP的销售税豁免仅使含税价格上升1.9%。在SNAP使用率更高的零售商处，支出下降幅度更大，并且在结账污名较不显著的线上渠道仍然很大。若在全国范围内推行，其自付负担仅为等效消费税的13%，而福利收益每年约达11亿美元。
