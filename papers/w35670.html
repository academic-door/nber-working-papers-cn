Air Pollution and Learning

空气污染与学习

A. Patrick Behrer, Joshua Goodman, J. Parker Goyer, R. Jisung Park

环境与气候 西班牙、美国

Abstract

Nearly the entire world's population breathes air exceeding WHO pollution guidelines, but the extent to which that exposure impairs the accumulation of human capital is not well understood. We study this using longitudinal PSAT data on nearly 10 million U.S. high school students, comparing the same student's scores across attempts preceded by differing air quality and instrumenting for local PM2.5 with smoke from distant wildfires. A year of observed pollution exposure reduces learning by 0.04-0.06 standard deviations, or 14-19% of typical annual score growth. The damage comes almost entirely from moderate pollution days (8–12 μg/m3), below the EPA's historical standard, and from exposure during the school year rather than summer, pointing to instructional disruption as a mechanism. Effects are three times larger in disadvantaged schools and among Black and Hispanic students, who are harmed more by the same exposure. Exposure to air pollution widens achievement gaps.

中文摘要

全球几乎所有人口呼吸的空气都超过了世界卫生组织的污染指南，但这种暴露在多大程度上损害人力资本的积累尚不十分清楚。我们利用近1000万名美国高中生的纵向PSAT数据对此进行研究，比较同一学生在不同空气质量条件下参加考试的成绩，并以远处野火产生的烟雾作为当地PM2.5的工具变量。一年的观测污染暴露使学习能力降低0.04-0.06个标准差，即典型年度成绩增长的14%-19%。这种损害几乎完全来自中度污染日（8-12微克/立方米），低于EPA的历史标准，且主要来自学年期间的暴露而非暑假，这表明教学中断是一个机制。在弱势学校以及黑人和西班牙裔学生中，影响是三倍之大，他们在相同暴露下受到的伤害更大。空气污染暴露扩大了成绩差距。
