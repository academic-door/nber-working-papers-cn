NBER Working Paper w36010所在周报

Gambling for Goals

为人生目标而赌博

Costas Arkolakis, Spencer Y. Kwon, Dominic Russel, Claire Shi, Hyunjoo Yang

金融与资产定价 韩国

Abstract

Young adults in advanced economies have grown more pessimistic about their economic prospects in recent years. At the same time, gambling-like financial activities have proliferated. We link these trends through a Friedman-Savage (1948) motive: when life goals such as homeownership or marriage seem out of reach, individuals turn to financial gambles. We test this theory using an original survey combined with millions of linked records from Korea’s leading credit bureau, telecom provider, and credit card issuer. Consistent with the theory, individuals who place greater importance on hard-to-reach goals take more risk, and the form of risk they take varies systematically with their distance from those goals. Within individuals, cryptocurrency app usage rises during unemployment and falls after winning the right to purchase price-regulated housing through a lottery. A calibration shows how hard-to-reach goals lead households to act more aggressively on their beliefs, amplifying the consequences of overoptimism.

中文摘要

近年来，发达经济体的年轻人对其经济前景变得更加悲观。与此同时，类似赌博的金融活动激增。我们通过Friedman-Savage（1948）动机将这两种趋势联系起来：当购房或结婚等人生目标似乎遥不可及时，个人会转向金融赌博。我们使用一项原创调查，并结合来自韩国领先征信机构、电信提供商和信用卡发行商的数百万条关联记录来检验这一理论。与该理论一致，更重视难以实现目标的个人承担更多风险，而且他们承担风险的形式随其与这些目标的距离而系统性变化。在个体内部，加密货币应用使用率在失业期间上升，而在通过抽签赢得购买价格管制住房的权利后下降。一项校准展示了难以实现的目标如何导致家庭更激进地依据其信念行事，从而放大过度乐观的后果。
