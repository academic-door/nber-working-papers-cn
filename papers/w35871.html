The Effects of Long-Term Exposure to Pollution on Health

长期暴露于污染对健康的影响

Joshua S. Graff Zivin, Johnny Huynh, Timothy Frank Justicz, Adriana Lleras-Muney, Matthew J. Neidell

环境与气候 美国

Abstract

We provide the first causal evidence on how long-term exposure to fine particulate matter (PM2.5) at contemporary ambient levels contributes to the development of chronic disease in a young, initially healthy population. Our analysis links detailed personnel records for roughly 150,000 U.S. Army soldiers to high-resolution annual measures of PM2.5. Because soldiers enter service exceptionally healthy and receive uniform access to care, new diagnoses reflect pollution’s physiological impact rather than preexisting conditions or differential health care access. They are also required to relocate based on the needs of the Army, which provides us with quasi-random variation in initial duty-station assignments that we use as an instrument for average annual pollution exposure over a soldier’s career. We find that sustained PM2.5 exposure substantially increases circulatory disease, particularly hypertension, with mixed evidence on respiratory conditions that are sensitive to specification. Exposure over shorter durations, by contrast, has little detectable effect, a pattern consistent with the cumulative nature of pollution’s health burden. These findings demonstrate that even moderate long-term exposure to air pollution can erode health capital in ways that are invisible to studies focused on short-run variations or mortality alone, and they highlight the importance of regulatory standards that account for chronic exposure risks.

中文摘要

我们提供了首个因果证据，说明在当代环境水平下长期暴露于细颗粒物（PM2.5）如何促成一个年轻且初始健康人群中慢性疾病的发展。我们的分析将大约150,000名美国陆军士兵的详细人事记录与高分辨率年度PM2.5测量数据相链接。由于士兵入伍时极为健康，并且获得统一的医疗服务，新诊断反映的是污染的生理影响，而不是既存疾病或医疗服务获取差异。他们还根据陆军需要被要求迁移，这为我们提供了初始驻地分配的准随机变异，我们将其用作士兵职业生涯中年均污染暴露的工具变量。我们发现，持续的PM2.5暴露显著增加循环系统疾病，尤其是高血压，而对呼吸系统疾病的证据则因设定不同而混合。相比之下，较短时间的暴露几乎没有可检测到的影响，这一模式与污染健康负担的累积性质一致。这些发现表明，即使是中等的长期空气污染暴露，也会以仅关注短期变化或死亡率的研究所看不见的方式侵蚀健康资本，并凸显了考虑慢性暴露风险的监管标准的重要性。
