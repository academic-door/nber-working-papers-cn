Wildfire, Smoke and Mental Health in Canada

加拿大野火、烟雾与心理健康

Janet Currie, Soodeh Saberian

环境与气候 加拿大

Abstract

Most previous estimates of the effects of wildfire on mental health have focused on the impact of exposure to PM2.5, which often travels long distances. We break new ground by highlighting additional impacts of wildfires on mental health, including through evacuation orders, local costs of fires, and climate anxiety, which is proxied using news reports of distant fires. All these mechanisms affect mental health-related hospitalizations, with especially large impacts on hospitalizations for anxiety and substance abuse. Conditional on local air quality, wildfire events that draw national attention worsen the mental health of susceptible people, even when they live far away. Elderly people and those with pre-existing health conditions that make them more vulnerable are more strongly affected. Accounting for these additional mechanisms does little to diminish the estimated effect of PM2.5 from wildfire smoke, but raises the estimated impact of fires on mental health 75%, suggesting that increases in PM2.5 are not the only important mechanism.

中文摘要

以往大多数关于野火对心理健康影响的估计都集中在PM2.5暴露的影响上，而PM2.5往往能远距离传播。我们开辟了新领域，强调了野火对心理健康的额外影响，包括通过疏散令、火灾的地方成本以及气候焦虑（以远处火灾的新闻报道作为代理指标）。所有这些机制都会影响与心理健康相关的住院，其中对焦虑和药物滥用住院的影响尤为显著。在控制当地空气质量的情况下，引起全国关注的野火事件会恶化易感人群的心理健康，即使他们居住在很远的地方。老年人以及那些因已有健康状况而更脆弱的人群受到的影响更为强烈。考虑这些额外机制并不会削弱对野火烟雾中PM2.5的估计效应，但会使野火对心理健康影响的估计值提高75%，这表明PM2.5的增加并非唯一重要的机制。
