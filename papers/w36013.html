Irregular Demand: The Anti-Compromise Effect in the Field

不规则需求：实地中的反妥协效应

Nicola Gennaioli, John A. List, Matthias Rodemeier, Gregory K. Sun

其他主题 未指明区域

Abstract

Neoclassical demand obeys regularity: adding a good can only draw demand away from existing goods. In a natural field experiment with Lyft covering 7.2 million riders and 47 million choices, we randomize the presence and price of a premium ride. Contrary to regularity, the premium ride raises demand for the cheapest incumbent and for not buying. The effect grows with the premium price and with repeated exposure, and persists in sessions where the premium ride is not displayed. Rational inference and inattention cannot account for these facts. A model in which a surprisingly high price, cued on the app and retrieved from memory, draws attention to prices explains them.

中文摘要

新古典需求遵循正则性：增加一种商品只能将需求从现有商品上引开。在一项覆盖720万乘客和4700万次选择的Lyft自然实地实验中，我们随机化高端乘车的存在和价格。与正则性相反，高端乘车提高了对最便宜现有选项和不购买的需求。该效应随高端价格和重复暴露而增大，并在未展示高端乘车的会话中持续存在。理性推断和注意力不足无法解释这些事实。一个模型可以解释这些现象：令人意外的高价格在应用上被提示并从记忆中提取，从而将注意力引向价格。
