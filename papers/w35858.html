Stressful Exposure from the 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami Drives Long-term Changes in Thyroid Hormone Physiology

2004年印度洋海啸带来的压力暴露推动甲状腺激素生理的长期变化

Ralph I. Lawton, Duncan Thomas, Elizabeth Frankenberg, Cecep Sumantri, Teresa Seeman, Eileen Crimmins, Daniel Hochbaum

健康与医疗 印度、印度尼西亚

Abstract

Stressful events are associated with long-term adverse health impacts, but the causal mechanisms linking exposures to disease remain poorly understood. One mechanism is persistent changes to hormonal signaling that regulate metabolic homeostasis. We examine the long-term impacts of stress associated with exposure to the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami on levels of free triiodothyronine (FT3), a circulating measure of biologically active thyroid hormone, using data from the Study of Tsunami Aftermath and Recovery. Biomarkers are measured 20 years post-tsunami in a population-representative sample age 35y+ in 2024 who, at the time of the tsunami, were living along the coast of Aceh, Indonesia, the area hardest-hit by the tsunami. Because respondents were first interviewed before the tsunami and subsequently tracked regardless of migration, the sample avoids selection bias common in long-term disaster studies. We identify the causal effect of tsunami exposure using variation in community-level tsunami mortality, comparing communities within the same sub-district. We find that greater tsunami exposure reduced levels of FT3 20 years later. Exposure also altered relationships between FT3, body composition, and cortisol and increased cardiometabolic risk. These findings identify persistent alteration of thyroid hormone physiology as a potential pathway linking severe stressful exposures to long-term cardiometabolic disease risk.

中文摘要

压力事件与长期不良健康影响相关，但将暴露与疾病联系起来的因果机制仍知之甚少。一种机制是调节代谢稳态的激素信号发生持续变化。我们使用海啸后果与恢复研究的数据，考察与2004年印度洋海啸暴露相关的压力对游离三碘甲状腺原氨酸（FT3）水平的长期影响，FT3是生物活性甲状腺激素的循环测量指标。生物标志物在海啸后20年测量，样本为2024年年龄35岁及以上、具有人群代表性的样本，这些人在海啸发生时居住在印度尼西亚亚齐海岸，该地区是受海啸打击最严重的地区。由于受访者在海啸前首次接受访谈，并在之后无论是否迁移都被追踪，该样本避免了长期灾害研究中常见的选择偏差。我们利用社区层面海啸死亡率的变化来识别海啸暴露的因果效应，并在同一街道内比较社区。我们发现，更大的海啸暴露降低了20年后的FT3水平。暴露还改变了FT3、身体成分和皮质醇之间的关系，并增加了心脏代谢风险。这些发现表明，甲状腺激素生理的持续改变可能是将严重压力暴露与长期心脏代谢疾病风险联系起来的潜在路径。
