Automation and Optimal Taxation: A Task-Based Theory

自动化与最优税收：基于任务的理论

Henrik Kleven, Owen M. Zidar

公共财政与税收 美国

Abstract

We characterize optimal labor and capital income taxation in a task-based model of automation. Workers and machines are perfect substitutes in automatable tasks, which run from the bottom of the skill distribution up to a threshold. Workers supply labor on the extensive margin. Capitalists supply machines at a finite elasticity. In this general-equilibrium automation economy, optimal tax formulas take canonical partial-equilibrium forms. Yet automation substantially changes optimal tax rates because wages and capital returns are endogenous. We calibrate the model to the US wage distribution and automation exposure. In equilibrium, the middle class is the most automated, with machine intensity peaking around the 40th wage percentile. Relative to a no-automation benchmark, the optimal labor tax schedule is more progressive: a larger EITC subsidy at the bottom, lower taxes in the middle, and higher taxes at the top. The optimal capital tax is sizable but unaffected by automation.

中文摘要

我们在基于任务的自动化模型中刻画最优劳动和资本所得税。工人和机器在可自动化任务中是完美替代品，这些任务从技能分布的底部延伸到某一阈值。工人在广延边际上供给劳动。资本家以有限弹性供给机器。在这个一般均衡自动化经济中，最优税收公式采取经典的部分均衡形式。然而，自动化大幅改变最优税率，因为工资和资本回报是内生的。我们将模型校准至美国工资分布和自动化暴露。在均衡中，中产阶级被自动化程度最高，机器强度在第40个工资百分位附近达到峰值。相对于无自动化基准，最优劳动税制更具累进性：底部更大的EITC补贴、中间更低的税收、顶部更高的税收。最优资本税相当可观，但不受自动化影响。
