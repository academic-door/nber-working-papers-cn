Approximating the Equilibrium Effects of Informed School Choice

近似信息化学择校的均衡效应

Claudia Allende, Francisco Gallego, Christopher Neilson

教育与劳动 智利

Abstract

We study how information about school quality affects families’ choices and how scaling up the intervention changes its impact. In a randomized trial in Chile, we offer families a short video and a personalized school report card before they enroll their children. Treated families choose schools with higher test scores and value added. Among families reached before enrollment, treated children score about 0.2 standard deviations higher in fourth grade, with positive but less precise estimates on later assessments. We embed the experimentally identified demand shift in a structural model of school choice and competition to study equilibrium effects at scale. Capacity constraints and centralized assignment attenuate the demand-side gains, but the same shift in demand strengthens schools’ incentives to improve quality, and the supply response roughly offsets the congestion effect. More generally, equilibrium scaling can alter experimental effects in either direction because congestion and endogenous quality responses work against each other. We use the model to guide the design of a follow-up cluster-randomized experiment, focusing on the tradeoff between precision and contamination from equilibrium spillovers.

中文摘要

我们研究关于学校质量的信息如何影响家庭选择，以及扩大该干预规模如何改变其影响。在智利的一项随机试验中，我们在家庭为孩子注册入学前，向他们提供一段短视频和一份个性化学校报告卡。接受处理的家庭选择了考试分数和增值更高的学校。在注册前接触到这些信息的家庭中，接受处理的儿童在四年级时的成绩约高出 0.2 个标准差，后续评估的估计为正但精度较低。我们将实验识别出的需求转移嵌入一个学校选择与竞争的结构模型中，以研究规模化后的均衡效应。容量约束和集中化分配削弱了需求侧收益，但同样的需求转移增强了学校提高质量的激励，而供给响应大致抵消了拥堵效应。更一般地，均衡规模化可能朝任一方向改变实验效应，因为拥堵和内生质量响应彼此反向作用。我们使用该模型指导一项后续整群随机实验的设计，重点关注精度与均衡溢出污染之间的权衡。
