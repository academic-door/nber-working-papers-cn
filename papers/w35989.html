Clearing the Fog: Price Transparency, Market Churn, and Consumer Welfare in Short-Term Rentals

拨开迷雾：短期租赁中的价格透明度、市场更替与消费者福利

Ginger Zhe Jin, Liad Wagman, Jingyi Xing

其他主题 未指明区域

Abstract

We study California's short-term lodging price transparency law (AB 537), which from July 1, 2024 required Airbnb and other providers to advertise fee-inclusive prices. Using Coarsened Exact Matching to compare Airbnb listings in San Diego and San Francisco with those in Seattle and Chicago, we find that all-in pricing made guests more price-sensitive relative to control cities; yet, contrary to the standard prediction that transparency lowers prices, average daily rates rose by 4.8 percent. We also observe significant market churn: relative to control cities, treated cities saw more exits and entries, listings reliant on cleaning-fee revenue disproportionately exited, higher-quality listings were more likely to stay, and entrants offered lower fees and lower quality than incumbents. A discrete-choice welfare analysis shows that the law removed a sizable “welfare illusion” created by obscured fees and, largely through market churn, raised realized consumer surplus. The net gains, after accounting for price increases, were positive on average but unevenly distributed across treated ZIP codes.

中文摘要

我们研究了加利福尼亚州短期住宿价格透明法（AB 537），该法自2024年7月1日起要求Airbnb和其他提供商宣传含费用的价格。使用粗化精确匹配将圣地亚哥和旧金山的Airbnb房源与西雅图和芝加哥的房源进行比较，我们发现全包定价使客人相对于对照城市对价格更敏感；然而，与透明度降低价格的标准预测相反，平均每日房价上涨了4.8%。我们还观察到显著的市场更替：相对于对照城市，处理城市出现了更多退出和进入，依赖清洁费收入的房源不成比例地退出，更高质量的房源更可能留下，而进入者提供的费用更低、质量也低于现有房源。离散选择福利分析表明，该法律消除了被隐藏费用造成的大规模“福利幻觉”，并主要通过市场更替提高了实现的消费者剩余。在计入价格上涨后，净收益平均为正，但在处理邮政编码之间分布不均。
