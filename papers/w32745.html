How Do Habits Form? Experimental Evidence from Health Screenings

习惯如何形成？来自健康筛查的实验证据

Damon Jones, David Molitor, Julian Reif

健康与医疗 未指明区域

Abstract

We develop a framework for distinguishing between two competing mechanisms of habit formation and apply it to a three-year randomized experiment on annual health screenings among 4,799 university employees. Annually re-randomized incentives generate exogenous variation in early, late, and repeated screening, allowing us to test whether habits are strengthened by additional consumption or instead form discretely once consumption crosses a threshold. We find strong habit formation from initial exposure: completing a first screening raised subsequent screening rates by 32.4–36.1 percentage points (90%–111%). In contrast, completing a second screening had no detectable additional effect. Our results reject reinforcement models, including addiction, which predict repeated participation should strengthen the habit, and instead support a threshold model in which a single exposure is sufficient to establish and sustain the habit. Observational estimates imply the opposite conclusion, showing the value of rigorous research design in identifying mechanisms of habit formation.

中文摘要

我们开发了一个框架来区分两种竞争性的习惯形成机制，并将其应用于一项针对4799名大学员工的年度健康筛查的三年随机实验。每年重新随机化的激励产生了早期、晚期和重复筛查的外生变化，使我们能够检验习惯是通过额外消费得到加强，还是在消费超过阈值后离散形成。我们发现初次接触会产生强烈的习惯形成：完成第一次筛查使后续筛查率提高了32.4至36.1个百分点（90%至111%）。相比之下，完成第二次筛查没有可检测到的额外效应。我们的结果拒绝了强化模型（包括成瘾模型，该模型预测重复参与应加强习惯），而是支持一个阈值模型，在该模型中，单次接触就足以建立和维持习惯。观察性估计暗示了相反的结论，显示了严谨的研究设计在识别习惯形成机制中的价值。
