Inside Central Bankers’ Calendars: How Do Fed Chairs Allocate Their Time?

央行行长的日历内幕：美联储主席如何分配时间？

Thomas Drechsel, Dali Tsakadze

环境与气候 未指明区域

Abstract

How leaders allocate their limited time and attention is an important question in economics. This paper constructs and analyzes a novel data set covering the daily calendars of three Federal Reserve Chairs, Ben Bernanke, Janet Yellen, and Jerome Powell, containing more than 20,000 calendar entries. We document several patterns. First, all Fed Chairs spend more than 30% of their documented meetings with Fed staff, highlighting the crucial role of economists in the Fed's decision process. Second, interactions with the media became more frequent during Bernanke's tenure and remained important until the end of Powell's tenure. Third, Powell held significantly more meetings with lawmakers than both Yellen and Bernanke, reflecting an increasing need for the Fed to engage with political actors. Beyond these trend patterns, we examine the time allocation of Fed Chairs at business cycle frequencies and show how different activities respond to shifts in the macroeconomic environment. Finally, we place our findings in historical context using the available calendars of earlier Fed Chairs, including Alan Greenspan.

中文摘要

领导者如何分配有限的时间和注意力是经济学中的一个重要问题。本文构建并分析了一个新颖的数据集，涵盖三位美联储主席本·伯南克、珍妮特·耶伦和杰罗姆·鲍威尔的每日日历，包含超过20,000条日历条目。我们记录了若干模式。第一，所有美联储主席将超过30%的有记录会议用于与美联储工作人员会面，凸显了经济学家在美联储决策过程中的关键作用。第二，与媒体的互动在伯南克任期内变得更加频繁，并一直重要到鲍威尔任期结束。第三，鲍威尔与立法者的会面显著多于耶伦和伯南克，反映出美联储与政治行为者接触的需求日益增加。除了这些趋势模式外，我们还考察了美联储主席在商业周期频率上的时间分配，并展示了不同活动如何响应宏观经济环境的变化。最后，我们利用包括艾伦·格林斯潘在内的早期美联储主席的可用日历，将我们的发现置于历史背景中。
