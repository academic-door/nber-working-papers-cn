Physicians' Occupational Licensing and the Quantity-Quality Trade-off

医生职业许可与数量-质量权衡

Juan Pablo Atal, Tomás Larroucau, Pablo Muñoz, Cristóbal Otero

国际贸易 智利

Abstract

Occupational licensing is a widespread quality regulation that potentially increases the quality of labor but reduces its quantity. We provide a theoretical framework to empirically quantify this trade-off in public hospitals, where both clinical quality and physician shortages are critical concerns. Using quasi-exogenous variation driven by a recent and unprecedented migration of physicians to Chile, together with growth in domestic medical schools, we show that more physicians improve patient outcomes, while lower-quality physicians—as measured by licensing exam performance—worsen them. Incorporating these findings into our theoretical framework, we evaluate the implications of locally changing the stringency of the licensing policy.

中文摘要

职业许可是一种广泛存在的质量监管，可能提高劳动力质量，但会减少其数量。我们提供了一个理论框架，以在公立医院中实证量化这种权衡，因为在这些医院中，临床质量和医生短缺都是关键问题。利用由近期前所未有的医生向智利迁移以及国内医学院增长所驱动的准外生变化，我们表明更多医生会改善患者结果，而质量较低的医生——以执照考试成绩衡量——则会使其恶化。将这些发现纳入我们的理论框架后，我们评估了局部改变许可政策严格程度的含义。
