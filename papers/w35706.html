Meetings

会议

David J. Deming, Katrine V. Løken, Alexander Willén, Yaling Xu

宏观与货币财政 挪威

Abstract

Why do we have so many meetings? Few workplace features are so scorned, yet seemingly so necessary. This paper provides the first large-scale economic evidence on workplace meetings using an original survey of more than 9,000 workers linked to matched employer–employee administrative data from Norway. We show that meetings are both common and costly, consuming an average of 12 percent of work hours and 14 percent of firm wage bills. Planning, problem solving, information sharing, and project coordination account for the majority of meeting activity. High-paying and high-revenue firms devote more resources to meetings despite facing a substantially higher opportunity cost of employee time. Meeting frequency and intensity are positively related to worker wage growth. Workers in meeting-intensive firms report greater on-the-job learning, and interactions with more senior colleagues are associated with stronger wage growth, suggesting that knowledge transmission within firms is an important mechanism. Meetings are the broccoli of work – widely disliked, but probably good for us anyway.

中文摘要

为什么我们有这么多会议？很少有职场特征如此受人诟病，却又看似必不可少。本文利用一项针对超过9,000名工人的原始调查，并将其与挪威匹配的雇主-雇员行政数据相关联，提供了关于职场会议的首个大规模经济学证据。我们表明，会议既普遍又代价高昂，平均消耗了12%的工作时间和14%的企业工资总额。规划、问题解决、信息共享和项目协调构成了会议活动的主体。高薪和高收入企业在会议上投入更多资源，尽管它们面临着显著更高的员工时间机会成本。会议频率和强度与工人工资增长呈正相关。会议密集型企业中的工人报告了更多的在职学习，而与更资深同事的互动与更强的工资增长相关，这表明企业内的知识传递是一个重要机制。会议是工作中的西兰花——普遍不受欢迎，但可能对我们有益。
