Rust in Motion: The Political Environmental Trap of Vehicle Tax Exemptions

锈迹斑斑的流动：车辆税收豁免的政治环境陷阱

Gabriel Facundes Monteiro, Edson R. Severnini

环境与气候 巴西

Abstract

Transportation is a major source of greenhouse gas and air pollution emissions worldwide, with most automobile pollution coming from vehicles older than 10 years. Yet, many policies discourage the removal of older vehicles from the fleet, with potential consequences for pollution and public health. This paper examines an equity-motivated anti-scrappage policy in Brazil using a border-pair design that exploits state variation in the age thresholds for vehicle ownership tax exemptions. Using municipality-level vehicle registration data from 2013-2020, we find that lower exemption age thresholds increase the average fleet age by up to one year, as they encourage owners to retain vehicles until they qualify for tax exemptions. Using fleet- and satellite-based estimates of pollution and administrative health data, we show that these policies increase on-road CO2 emissions per capita by up to 23% and PM2.5 concentrations by 4%, while deteriorating infant health outcomes. Leveraging state reforms that raised exemption ages, we find evidence that incumbents face greater electoral penalties wherever a greater share of the vehicle fleet re-enters the tax base, consistent with a “political environmental trap.” Within an MVPF framework, we find that lowering the exemption age by five years generates a net welfare loss of $0.78 for every $1 of forgone government revenue.

中文摘要

交通运输是全球温室气体和空气污染的主要来源之一，而大部分汽车污染来自车龄超过10年的车辆。然而，许多政策不鼓励老旧车辆退出车队，这可能对污染和公共健康产生影响。本文利用巴西一项基于公平动机的反报废政策，采用边界配对设计，利用各州车辆保有税豁免年龄门槛的差异。利用2013-2020年市级车辆登记数据，我们发现，较低的豁免年龄门槛会使平均车队车龄增加最多一年，因为这鼓励车主保留车辆直至其符合税收豁免资格。利用基于车队和卫星的污染估算以及行政健康数据，我们表明这些政策使人均道路二氧化碳排放量增加高达23%，PM2.5浓度增加4%，同时恶化了婴儿健康结局。利用提高豁免年龄的州级改革，我们发现，在车辆重新纳入税基比例越高的地方，现任者面临的选举惩罚越大，这与“政治环境陷阱”相一致。在MVPF框架内，我们发现将豁免年龄降低五年，每放弃1美元的政府收入会产生0.78美元的净福利损失。
