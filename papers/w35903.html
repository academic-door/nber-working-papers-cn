From Politics to Process: Evidence from Progressive Era Naturalizations

从政治到程序：来自进步时代归化入籍的证据

Abhay Aneja, Guo Xu

国际贸易 美国

Abstract

States that delegate governance to local officials face a trade-off between the benefits of local knowledge and the costs of biased administrators. Can imposing federal standards discipline bias and improve uniformity? We study this question by examining the introduction of federal standards to apply for citizenship under the 1906 Naturalization Act. Using newly-digitized census records covering over five million immigrants and a 30-year period, we show that standardizing adjudication of citizenship applications resulted in rapid convergence of naturalization practices across cities. While naturalizations were around 30% higher in election years pre-reform, this electoral cycle disappeared after the introduction of federal standards. Exploiting the staggered adoption of municipal civil service reforms aimed at removing patronage during the Progressive Era, we find that the reduction in political cycles was strongest in unreformed cities. Finally, the contraction in election year naturalizations changed the composition of new citizens. Affected cohorts earned 7% more, were more likely to enter citizenship-restricted occupations, and gave children more Americanized names. Our results thus show that standardizing administrative procedures can discipline politically-biased policy implementation.

中文摘要

将治理委托给地方官员的州面临地方知识收益与有偏管理者成本之间的权衡。施加联邦标准能否约束偏见并提高统一性？我们通过考察1906年《归化法》下申请公民身份的联邦标准的引入来研究这一问题。使用覆盖超过500万移民和30年期间的新数字化人口普查记录，我们表明，公民身份申请裁决的标准化导致各城市归化实践迅速趋同。改革前选举年的归化入籍率约高出30%，但在引入联邦标准后这一选举周期消失了。利用进步时代旨在消除恩庇制的市政公务员改革的分批采纳，我们发现政治周期的减少在未改革城市中最强。最后，选举年归化入籍的收缩改变了新公民的构成。受影响 cohort 的收入高出7%，更可能进入限制公民身份的职业，并给孩子起更美国化的名字。因此，我们的结果表明，标准化行政程序可以约束政治偏倚的政策执行。
