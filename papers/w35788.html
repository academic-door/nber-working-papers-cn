NBER Working Paper w35788所在周报

Beliefs Over Contracts

关于合同的信念

Francis Annan, Collin B. Raymond

教育与劳动 未指明区域

Abstract

We study how firms choose among incentive contracts and how accurately managers predict their effects. Using a survey of managerial beliefs and a large-scale field experiment, we randomly assign agents at the market level to several widely used, expenditure-equivalent incentive schemes. In the field, the best-performing contract raises weekly firm profit by over 55% relative to the status quo, despite being ranked lower by managers, whereas the worst-performing contract performs as predicted. Managers reliably avoid poorly performing contracts but systematically underestimate top-performing ones. Standard contract theory does better as a forecaster here: an unbiased textbook principal orders the contracts correctly, or very nearly so, correctly identifying the profit leader where managers do not; where it does miss, the error is similar to managers’ and for a similar reason. We show that the performance differences across contracts reflect a mix of labor-supply and risk responses, and that managerial predictability varies with contract complexity and managerial hierarchy. Our results highlight the importance of contract design for firm performance and reveal systematic limits to managerial cognition in shaping incentives.

中文摘要

我们研究企业如何在激励合同之间进行选择，以及管理者对其效果的预测有多准确。利用一项管理者信念调查和一项大规模实地实验，我们在市场层面将代理人随机分配到几种广泛使用、支出等价的激励方案中。在实地中，表现最好的合同使企业每周利润相对于现状提高超过55%，尽管管理者对其排名较低，而表现最差的合同表现如预测。管理者可靠地避开表现不佳的合同，但系统性地低估表现最好的合同。标准合同理论在这里作为预测器表现更好：一个无偏的教科书式委托人正确或非常接近正确地排列合同，正确识别出利润领先者，而管理者则不能；在出错之处，错误与管理者相似，原因也相似。我们表明，合同之间的表现差异反映了劳动供给和风险反应的混合，并且管理者的可预测性随合同复杂性和管理层级而变化。我们的结果凸显了合同设计对企业绩效的重要性，并揭示了管理者认知在塑造激励方面的系统性局限。
