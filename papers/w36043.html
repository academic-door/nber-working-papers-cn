NBER Working Paper w36043所在周报

Mental Models of Fiscal Policy

财政政策的心理模型

Francesco Bianchi, Era Dabla-Norris, Radhika Goyal, Victoria Haver, Salma Khalid

宏观与货币财政 全球/多国

Abstract

We study how individuals form expectations about fiscal adjustment. Using survey data from more than 27,000 respondents across 13 countries, we jointly elicit beliefs about current fiscal conditions, expectations of tax increases and spending cuts, and open-ended explanations for those expectations. We classify these explanations into economic mental models, political narratives, and experience-based heuristics. Respondents reporting similar fiscal conditions nevertheless form systematically different expectations of adjustment. Economic mental models are associated with stronger expectations of tax increases and spending cuts in the fiscal configurations we examine, whereas experience-based reasoning is associated with weaker adjustment expectations. These differences are also reflected in intended economic behavior and portfolio allocations. In a randomized experiment, fiscal information increases the likelihood that respondents articulate economic reasoning, primarily among responses that would otherwise fall outside the three primary frameworks.

中文摘要

我们研究个体如何形成对财政调整的预期。利用来自13个国家超过27,000名受访者的调查数据，我们联合 elicits 对当前财政状况的信念、对增税和削减支出的预期，以及对这些预期的开放式解释。我们将这些解释分类为经济心理模型、政治叙事和基于经验的启发式。报告相似财政状况的受访者 nevertheless 形成了系统不同的调整预期。在我们考察的财政配置中，经济心理模型与更强的增税和削减支出预期相关联，而基于经验的推理与较弱的调整预期相关联。这些差异也反映在预期的经济行为和投资组合配置中。在一项随机实验中，财政信息提高了受访者阐述经济推理的可能性，主要是在那些原本会落在三个主要框架之外的回答中。
