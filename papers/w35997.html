Beyond Overdose Prevention: Naloxone Coprescription Laws and the Path to Treatment

超越过量预防：纳洛酮共同处方法律与通往治疗之路

Shin-Yi Chou, Jiarun Li, Bingjin Xue

教育与劳动 美国

Abstract

Over one-third of U.S. states have enacted naloxone coprescription laws, requiring physicians to prescribe or offer naloxone alongside high-risk opioids. Using Medicare Part D data, the Treatment Episode Data Set, and a difference-in-differences design, we study how these laws affect naloxone access, broader prescription behavior, and treatment engagement. Coprescription laws increased naloxone prescribing by 275%, with effects twice as large in states requiring prescriptions as in those requiring only offers. We find no evidence of increased opioid or benzodiazepine prescribing, countering moral hazard concerns that broader naloxone access might encourage riskier opioid use. Instead, we document significant increases in prescribing of medications for opioid use disorder (MOUD) and gains in opioid use disorder treatment admissions, primarily in deliberate treatment-seeking settings. These results suggest that coprescription laws may have broader benefits beyond overdose prevention.

中文摘要

美国超过三分之一的州已颁布纳洛酮共同处方法律，要求医生在开具高风险阿片类药物时同时开具或提供纳洛酮。利用Medicare Part D数据、治疗事件数据集以及双重差分设计，我们研究这些法律如何影响纳洛酮获取、更广泛的处方行为以及治疗参与。共同处方法律使纳洛酮处方量增加了275%，在要求开具处方的州，效果是仅要求提供纳洛酮的州的两倍。我们没有发现阿片类药物或苯二氮䓬类药物处方增加的证据，这反驳了更广泛的纳洛酮获取可能鼓励更高风险阿片类药物使用的道德风险担忧。相反，我们记录了阿片类药物使用障碍药物（MOUD）处方的显著增加以及阿片类药物使用障碍治疗入院人数的增长，主要发生在有意寻求治疗的环境中。这些结果表明，共同处方法律可能具有超越过量预防的更广泛益处。
