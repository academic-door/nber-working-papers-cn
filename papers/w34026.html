How Do Certificate-of-Need Laws Affect Hospitals? A Review of the Evidence

需求证明法如何影响医院？证据综述

Charles J. Courtemanche, Joseph Garuccio

健康与医疗 未指明区域

Abstract

Certificate-of-Need (CON) laws require the approval of states’ health planning agencies for health care providers to engage in regulated actions such as opening or expanding facilities or purchasing equipment. Such laws initially aimed to restrain health care costs and wasteful redundancies, but they are also now seen by advocates as helping to preserve the financial viability of struggling hospitals. Opponents of CON laws argue that they stifle competition, leading to inflated costs and limited options for accessing care.\n\nThis study reviews the literature on the impacts of CON laws. All else equal, economic theory suggests that hospitals should see fewer new competitors, charge higher prices, and earn higher net revenues as a result of CON laws. Reduced competition can also weaken incentives to operate efficiently and deliver high quality care. However, these predictions assume otherwise perfectly competitive markets, and health care markets are heavily distorted in ways that change meaningfully over time. The need for new entrants to obtain regulatory approval could conceivably improve their quality of care while also helping to ensure that safety-net hospitals do not need to shut down unprofitable departments or close completely. Further, CON laws restrain not only entry of new facilities but also expansion of existing facilities, limiting their ability to leverage potential regulatory advantages.\n\nViewed in its totality, the available evidence supports some of the predictions of conventional economic theory but not others. In at least some cases, CON laws restrict both entry of new competitors and expansion of existing hospitals. The reduction in competitors increases the number of procedures per hospital. However, there is little evidence that this translates to increased prices or higher hospital profitability, and hardly any research tests for reduced closure rates. Studies on hospital efficiency and quality of care for procedures performed exclusively at hospitals mostly point to null or negative effects, but evidence on quality is more favorable for services that can be provided outside of hospitals.\n\nWe conclude that, despite the fact that the literature on CON laws is already large, more research is needed. Future work should utilize the latest tools for identifying causal effects and better account for the wide variation in CON laws across states.

中文摘要

需求证明法要求医疗保健提供者在进行受监管的行动（如开设或扩建设施、购买设备）之前，必须获得州卫生规划机构的批准。此类法律最初旨在控制医疗成本和浪费性重复，但现在也被倡导者视为有助于维持陷入困境医院的财务生存能力。反对者则认为需求证明法抑制竞争，导致成本膨胀和就医选择受限。 本研究回顾了关于需求证明法影响的文献。在其他条件相同的情况下，经济理论表明，由于需求证明法，医院应面临更少的新竞争者、收取更高的价格并获得更高的净收入。竞争减少也会削弱高效运营和提供高质量护理的激励。然而，这些预测假设市场是完全竞争的，而医疗保健市场受到严重扭曲，且这些扭曲随时间显著变化。新进入者需要获得监管批准，理论上可以提高其护理质量，同时有助于确保安全网医院无需关闭亏损部门或完全关闭。此外，需求证明法不仅限制新设施的进入，也限制现有设施的扩张，从而限制了它们利用潜在监管优势的能力。 总体来看，现有证据支持传统经济理论的部分预测，但不支持其他预测。至少在某些情况下，需求证明法既限制了新竞争者的进入，也限制了现有医院的扩张。竞争者减少增加了每家医院的手术量。然而，几乎没有证据表明这转化为更高的价格或更高的医院盈利能力，而且几乎没有研究检验其是否降低了关闭率。关于仅在医院进行的程序的医院效率和护理质量的研究大多指向无效或负面影响，但对于可以在医院外提供的服务，关于质量的证据更为有利。 我们得出结论，尽管关于需求证明法的文献已经很多，但仍需更多研究。未来的工作应利用最新的工具来识别因果效应，并更好地考虑各州需求证明法的广泛差异。
