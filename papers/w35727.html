Measuring Racial Disparities in Police Stops: Evidence from Telemetric Mobility Data

衡量警察拦截中的种族差异：来自遥测移动数据的证据

Xiaofeng Gong, Yuling Han, Susan T. Parker, Matthew B. Ross, Stephen Ross

其他主题 未指明区域

Abstract

We establish a reliable counterfactual for evaluating racial disparities in traffic stops using telemetric data to estimate the racial composition of motorists. We measure of motorist racial composition using telemetric mobility data and a novel two-stage correction methodology anchored by an independent measure, racial composition of motorists involved in accidents. Applying this to Massachusetts State Police stops, we find non-White motorists are stopped at rates exceeding their roadway presence by 6.7 percentage points. We demonstrate that uncalibrated telemetric data significantly understates minority presence (15.2 vs. 28 percent), and the uncalibrated telemetric data yields disparities that are over twice the size of our preferred estimates. We use this validated measure to assess the accuracy of several commonly-used disparity tests. We find that the widely used “Community Standard” test dramatically overestimates disparities, even when restricting to local roads or non-commuting hours. In contrast, the less-often used “Crash Benchmark” accurately captures disparities even when aggregated to higher temporal levels covering up to 94 percent of stops. Finally, the “Veil of Darkness” test yields smaller estimates than our telemetric measure, consistent with its focus on disparate treatment rather than the broader legal standard of motorist composition.

中文摘要

我们利用遥测数据估计驾驶者的种族构成，从而为评估交通拦截中的种族差异建立一个可靠的反事实。我们使用遥测移动数据和一种新颖的两阶段校正方法，并以一个独立测量指标——事故中驾驶者的种族构成——作为锚定，来测量驾驶者的种族构成。将其应用于马萨诸塞州警察的拦截数据，我们发现非白人驾驶者被拦截的比例比其在道路上的出现率高出6.7个百分点。我们证明，未经校准的遥测数据显著低估了少数族裔的出现率（15.2%对28%），并且未经校准的遥测数据得出的差异是我们首选估计值的两倍以上。我们使用这一经过验证的测量指标来评估几种常用差异检验的准确性。我们发现，广泛使用的“社区标准”检验极大地高估了差异，即使仅限于地方道路或非通勤时段也是如此。相比之下，较少使用的“事故基准”检验即使在汇总到覆盖高达94%拦截的更高时间层级时，也能准确捕捉差异。最后，“黑暗之幕”检验得出的估计值小于我们的遥测测量值，这与其关注差别对待而非驾驶者构成这一更广泛法律标准相一致。
