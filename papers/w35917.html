The Effects of a Shorter Workweek on Employment, Output, and Productivity

缩短工作周对就业、产出和生产率的影响

Pauline Carry, Claire Montialoux, Elio Nimier-David, Alexandra Roulet, Nina Roussille

教育与劳动 法国

Abstract

Do shorter workweeks generate work-sharing and productivity gains, as intended by policymakers? We study France’s pay-preserving reduction in the workweek from 39 to 35 hours, bundled with employer payroll tax cuts. We first exploit the staggered implementation by firm size to identify the impact of the reform package. We then use differential exposure to the hours cut and tax cut to separate their effects. We combine matched employer-employee, balance-sheet, and survey data to study worker, firm, and local labor market effects. The reform package left employment unchanged. This zero masks large offsetting effects: absent the tax cuts, the shorter workweek would have reduced employment by 3.8%. The effects are skill-biased, raising employment among managers and professionals while reducing it among other employees. Finally, shorter hours raised total factor productivity by 2%, limiting the output decline to 1% despite a 6% fall in total hours. Surveys point to work intensification and organizational changes as potential drivers of productivity gains.

中文摘要

缩短工作周是否会如政策制定者所期望的那样产生工作分享和生产率收益？我们研究法国在保持工资不变的情况下将工作周从39小时缩短至35小时，并配套雇主工资税减免。我们首先利用按企业规模分阶段实施来识别这一改革组合的影响。然后，我们利用对工时削减和税收减免的差异化暴露来分离其效果。我们结合匹配的雇主—雇员数据、资产负债表数据和调查数据，研究工作、企业和地方劳动力市场层面的影响。该改革组合使就业保持不变。这一零效应掩盖了巨大的抵消性效应：如果没有税收减免，缩短工作周本会使就业减少3.8%。这些效应具有技能偏向性，提高了管理者和专业人员的就业，同时减少了其他雇员的就业。最后，缩短工时使全要素生产率提高2%，尽管总工时下降6%，却将产出下降限制在1%。调查表明，工作强度上升和组织变革可能是生产率收益的潜在驱动因素。
