Pharmaceutical Pricing and R&D as a Global Public Good

作为全球公共品的药品定价与研发

H. E. Frech, III, Mark V. Pauly, William S. Comanor, Joseph R. Martinez

健康与医疗 英国、美国

Abstract

In his Labor Day address, President Biden stated that the U.S. “has the highest drug prices in the world, and there is no reason for it.” For new branded drugs, the first part of that statement is supported by a recent RAND Report (Mulcahy et. al. 2021) which found U.S. average prices are 2.3 times those present in both the 32 OECD countries overall and in the UK separately. In this research, we consider the second part of that statement, and identify the economic factors that suggest some “reasons for it.”\nViewing pharmaceutical markets through the lens of the theories of global public goods and alliances, as developed by Olson and Zeckhauser (1966), we explain the observed pricing differences along with their implications the for the global supply of innovative new drugs. Similar views were advanced in two U.S. government reports (CEA 2018, 2020), and also by Goldman and Lakdawala (2018). We develop these ideas further and implement them empirically.\nA commonly held theory presumes that drug companies in the U.S set prices for patented drugs at profit- maximizing levels that fund and incentivize substantial research and development efforts. In contrast, in the rest of the world (ROW), national authorities set prices minimally above marginal costs of production, allowing few revenues remaining to support R&D (CEA Report 2018; Blumenthal 2018; Hooper and Henderson 2022). The ROW countries are then considered to be fully free riding on U.S. research efforts. We examine this argument both theoretically and empirically, and find it wanting. \nWe apply global public good theory to examine the pricing of branded drugs. To this end, we describe the optimal global contribution, as supported by the Lindahl pricing model, and show theoretically that existing independently determined contributions and thereby aggregate R&D levels are likely sub-optimal. Then we implement the model by calculating the contribution to the global public good as represented by short-term profits or quasi-rents received from sales of all branded drugs. These calculations are derived from pricing data contained in the RAND Report along with two market-based estimates of marginal costs. \nWe find that, while ROW contributions are less than those found in the United States, they are more than minimal, and do not approach zero for most countries. When we regress these positive contributions on a country’s size of GDP along with various controls, we find that GDP size alone is a powerful determinant of national contributions. It remains economically and statistically significant without regard to the controls introduced. In addition, we estimate how large are the contributions of ROW countries to the global public good. We offer reasons why US pharmaceutical prices and contributions per capita are nevertheless higher than those found in all ROW countries. We also suggest actions aimed to promote R&D efforts that are closer to the global optimum.

中文摘要

拜登总统在劳动节演讲中表示，美国“拥有世界上最高的药品价格，这毫无道理。”对于新的品牌药，该陈述的第一部分得到了兰德公司近期一份报告（Mulcahy等人，2021年）的支持，该报告发现美国平均价格是32个OECD国家整体以及单独英国价格的2.3倍。在本研究中，我们考察该陈述的第二部分，并识别出表明存在某些“理由”的经济因素。 通过奥尔森和泽克豪泽（1966）发展的全球公共品和联盟理论视角来审视药品市场，我们解释了观察到的价格差异及其对全球创新药物供应的含义。两份美国政府报告（CEA 2018, 2020）以及戈德曼和拉克达瓦拉（2018）也提出了类似观点。我们进一步深化这些想法并进行了实证检验。 一种普遍持有的理论假设，美国制药公司为专利药设定利润最大化的价格，这些价格资助并激励了大量的研发工作。相比之下，在世界其他地区（ROW），国家当局将价格设定在略高于生产边际成本的水平，几乎没有剩余收入来支持研发（CEA报告2018；布卢门撒尔2018；胡珀和亨德森2022）。因此，ROW国家被认为完全是在搭美国研发努力的便车。我们从理论和实证两方面审视了这一论点，并发现其存在不足。 我们应用全球公共品理论来研究品牌药的定价。为此，我们描述了由林达尔定价模型支持的最优全球贡献，并从理论上表明，现有的独立决定的贡献以及由此产生的总研发水平可能是次优的。然后，我们通过计算以所有品牌药销售所获得的短期利润或准租金形式体现的全球公共品贡献来实施该模型。这些计算基于兰德公司报告中的定价数据以及两个基于市场的边际成本估计。 我们发现，虽然ROW国家的贡献低于美国，但它们并非微不足道，并且对大多数国家而言远非零。当我们将这些正向贡献对一个国家的GDP规模以及各种控制变量进行回归时，我们发现GDP规模本身是国家贡献的一个强有力的决定因素。无论引入何种控制变量，它在经济上和统计上仍然显著。此外，我们估算了ROW国家对全球公共品的贡献有多大。我们提出了美国药品价格和人均贡献仍然高于所有ROW国家的原因。我们还建议采取旨在促进更接近全球最优水平的研发努力的行动。
