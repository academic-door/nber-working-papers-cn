Beyond Publication Bias: Characterizing and Understanding Missing Results in Economics

超越发表偏倚：刻画和理解经济学中缺失的结果

Fernando Hoces de la Guardia, Edward Miguel, Gufran Pathan, Akash Shaji, Viviane Helena Silva da Rocha, Erik Ø. Sørensen, Bertil Tungodden

其他主题 美国

Abstract

What scientists choose not to publish can be as important as what they do publish. We measure missing research results at the hypothesis level and use this approach to study under-reporting in economics. Across 5,057 pre-registered hypotheses from 317 studies registered on the American Economic Association RCT Registry between 2015 and 2017, only 42% have publicly available results eight to nine years after pre-registration. Among the available results, 64% are null, and we find little evidence that null results are disproportionately missing. In an RCT, direct engagement with authors increases the share of hypotheses with available results by 6.2 percentage points, while raising awareness alone has no significant effect and additional research assistance provides no further improvement. Direct engagement also increases explanations for unavailable results, reducing the share of hypotheses that remain unaccounted for by 16.4 percentage points. The findings document extensive under-reporting in economics that does not appear to be driven primarily by the suppression of null results, and suggest that reporting pre-registered results may need to become a standard part of the research process rather than something recovered years later.

中文摘要

科学家选择不发表的内容可能与他們发表的内容同样重要。我们在假设层面衡量缺失的研究结果，并利用这一方法研究经济学中的报告不足问题。在2015年至2017年间在美国经济学会RCT注册中心注册的317项研究的5,057个预注册假设中，只有42%在预注册后八到九年有公开可用的结果。在可用的结果中，64%为零结果，我们几乎没有发现零结果被不成比例地缺失的证据。在一项RCT中，与作者直接接触使有可用结果的假设比例提高了6.2个百分点，而仅提高意识没有显著效果，额外提供研究协助也没有进一步改善。直接接触还增加了对不可用结果的解释，使仍未得到说明的假设比例降低了16.4个百分点。这些发现记录了经济学中广泛的报告不足现象，而这一现象似乎并非主要由对零结果的压制所驱动，并表明报告预注册结果可能需要成为研究过程的标准组成部分，而不是多年后才被找回的东西。
