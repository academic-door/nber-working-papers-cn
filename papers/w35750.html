Informed Choices: Gender Gaps in Career Advice

知情选择：职业建议中的性别差距

Yana Gallen, Melanie Wasserman

教育与劳动 未指明区域

Abstract

This paper provides the first causal evidence that gender affects the information an individual receives about careers. We conduct a large-scale field experiment in which real college students seek career information from 10,000 working professionals. We randomize whether a professional receives a message from a male or a female student. When students ask broadly for information about a career, female students receive substantially more information on work/life balance than male students. This gender difference persists when students specifically ask about work/life balance. A survey of professionals suggests non-altruistic motives for discussing work/life balance with women. Combining findings from the field experiment and results from an information intervention, we conclude that gender gaps in information received about work/life balance are consequential for gender gaps in career intentions.

中文摘要

本文提供了首个因果证据，表明性别影响个人所获得的职业信息。我们进行了一项大规模实地实验，真实大学生向10,000名在职专业人士寻求职业信息。我们随机化专业人士收到的是来自男学生还是女学生的信息。当学生广泛询问职业信息时，女学生收到的关于工作与生活平衡的信息显著多于男学生。当学生专门询问工作与生活平衡时，这种性别差异依然存在。一项针对专业人士的调查表明，与女性讨论工作与生活平衡存在非利他动机。结合实地实验的发现和信息干预的结果，我们得出结论：所收到的工作与生活平衡信息中的性别差距对职业意向中的性别差距具有重要影响。
