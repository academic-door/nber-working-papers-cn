Labor Supply and Wages over the Life Cycle: Human Capital and Disability Benefits

生命周期中的劳动供给与工资：人力资本与残疾福利

Richard Blundell, Monica Costa-Dias, Graham Mazeine, Costas Meghir, Tom Waters

健康与医疗 英国

Abstract

Work experience has been found to be a key determinant of work and wages for educated women, but it seems to play a smaller role for the less educated. For them, welfare, tax credits and family composition matter more, and increasingly so does participation in disability programmes. Here we document how labor supply, wages, health, and disability claims have evolved in the UK over the last three decades, and examine how reforms to tax credits, disability benefits and other welfare programs have changed work incentives over this period. We then introduce these features into an empirical dynamic structural model of life-cycle labor supply, human capital and disability benefit application, in which human capital combines health capital, experience accumulated in work, and education. Estimating the model on a panel of working-age women in the UK, we examine the interactions between experience capital and health capital in driving life-cycle earnings across different education groups. Our results highlight the importance of interactions in the disability benefit, welfare and tax-credit systems for understanding the life cycle pattern of hours, employment, earnings, and disability claims, and for evaluating policy counterfactuals.

中文摘要

研究发现，工作经历是受教育女性工作和工资的关键决定因素，但对受教育程度较低者而言，其作用似乎较小。对她们来说，福利、税收抵免和家庭构成更重要，而且残疾项目的参与也越来越重要。本文记录了英国过去三十年中劳动供给、工资、健康和残疾申请如何演变，并考察税收抵免、残疾福利和其他福利项目改革如何改变了这一时期的工作激励。随后，我们将这些特征引入一个关于生命周期劳动供给、人力资本和残疾福利申请的实证动态结构模型，其中人力资本结合了健康资本、工作中积累的经验以及教育。我们在英国工作年龄女性面板上估计该模型，考察经验资本与健康资本在驱动不同教育群体生命周期收入方面的相互作用。我们的结果凸显了残疾福利、福利和税收抵免体系中的相互作用，对于理解小时数、就业、收入和残疾申请的生命周期模式以及评估政策反事实的重要性。
