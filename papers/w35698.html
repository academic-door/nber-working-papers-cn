Is a Dollar a Dollar? How Transfer Design Shapes Household Spending

一美元就是一美元吗？转移设计如何影响家庭支出

Therese Bonomo, Krista J. Ruffini, Diane Whitmore Schanzenbach

公共财政与税收 未指明区域

Abstract

Government transfers vary along two design dimensions that standard models predict should not matter: whether benefits are paid in cash or kind, and whether they arrive as one-time or recurring payments. We test how these design choices affect household spending using sharp changes in four pandemic-era transfers to low-income families with children: one-time food vouchers, monthly food benefits, one-time cash payments, and monthly cash payments. Linking variation in benefit timing and amount to high-frequency household scanner data, we estimate spending responses in a harmonized difference-in-differences and event-study framework. Households spend more out of in-kind benefits than out of cash transfers of similar value, and more out of recurring than one-time transfers in the short run. Our estimates produce the first internally comparable set of short-run food-store MPCs across transfer types, ranging from 0.18 (one-time) and 0.38 (monthly) for in-kind benefits, versus 0.06 (one-time) and 0.20 (monthly) for cash. The patterns are consistent with mental accounting, labeling, and forward-looking responses. More broadly, transfer design—not just generosity and targeting—is an important policy lever for social insurance and short-run demand stabilization.

中文摘要

政府转移支付在两个设计维度上有所不同，而标准模型预测这些维度不应产生影响：福利是以现金还是实物支付，以及它们是作为一次性还是经常性支付到达。我们利用疫情期间针对有子女低收入家庭的四种转移支付的急剧变化来测试这些设计选择如何影响家庭支出：一次性食品券、月度食品福利、一次性现金支付和月度现金支付。将福利时间和金额的变化与高频家庭扫描数据联系起来，我们在统一的双重差分和事件研究框架中估计支出反应。家庭从实物福利中支出的比例高于同等价值的现金转移，并且在短期内，经常性转移的支出比例高于一次性转移。我们的估计首次产生了一套跨转移类型在短期内具有内部可比性的食品店边际消费倾向（MPC），实物福利的范围从0.18（一次性）到0.38（月度），而现金的范围从0.06（一次性）到0.20（月度）。这些模式与心理账户、标签效应和前瞻性反应一致。更广泛地说，转移设计——不仅仅是慷慨程度和瞄准性——是社会保险和短期需求稳定的重要政策杠杆。
