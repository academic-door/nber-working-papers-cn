How Heavily Are PhD Students Offloading Their Scientific Writing to AI? Evidence from US STEM PhD Dissertations

博士生在多大程度上将科学写作外包给AI？来自美国STEM博士论文的证据

Daniel P. Gross, Dror Shvadron, Hansen Zhang

AI 与数字经济 美国

Abstract

Large language models are diffusing rapidly into science, raising concerns that delegating research writing to AI may undermine the development of expertise that scientific training is supposed to build. We measure AI-generated writing in the dissertations of a near-census of US STEM PhD graduates from 2019 to 2026 and link graduates to their subsequent employment. AI writing is absent before 2023, present in 29% of dissertations filed in 2026, and rapidly growing. Use is more common among students from non-English-speaking countries and at lower-ranked programs. Among graduates in the same program and cohort, those whose dissertations contain AI writing are less likely to enter academic research careers and more likely to enter non-tenure-track roles or industry, with this pattern strongest among US-origin students. At the same time as AI may increase research productivity, it thus appears to also be displacing a potentially important portion of the work through which scientific expertise is developed.

中文摘要

大型语言模型正在迅速扩散到科学领域，引发担忧：将研究写作委托给AI可能削弱科学训练本应建立的专业能力。我们测量了2019年至2026年美国STEM博士毕业生近乎全体的博士论文中由AI生成的写作，并将毕业生与其后续就业相链接。AI写作在2023年之前不存在，在2026年提交的博士论文中有29%存在，且正在快速增长。来自非英语国家的学生以及排名较低项目的学生使用更常见。在同一项目和同一届毕业生中，其博士论文包含AI写作的人更不可能进入学术研究职业，更可能进入非终身轨岗位或产业界，这一模式在美国本土学生中最强。在AI可能提高研究生产率的同时，它似乎也正在取代一部分对科学专业能力发展可能至关重要的工作。
