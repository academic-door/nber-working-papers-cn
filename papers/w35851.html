Latent Human Capital: How Early Investments in Parents Buffer Against Future Schooling Shocks

潜在人力资本：对父母的早期投资如何缓冲未来的学校教育冲击

Shruti Jha, John A. List, Rebecca C. Royer, Anya Samek

教育与劳动 未指明区域

Abstract

Do early investments in parents pay off even when treatment effects for children appear to fade out quickly? We answer this question by leveraging a field experiment in which households with 3-5 year-old children were randomized into a parenting program, a preschool, or a control group, combined with a natural experiment (Covid-19 school closures) that occurred ten years later. Prior to the closures, treatment effects on academic achievement had faded to zero across all groups. Yet, with Covid-19 school closures, effects of the parenting program re-emerge: test scores in control and preschool groups drop by roughly 0.25-0.30 SD, whereas scores in the parenting group drop by only 0.11 SD (difference: p = 0.01). Our findings suggest that parenting programs can generate latent human capital that only becomes visible when complementary school inputs are removed. This result is consonant with parental and school investments acting as substitutes in skill production. Because this latent parental capacity pays off precisely when school inputs fail, standard measurement tools for early childhood programs can miss crucial state-contingent value.

中文摘要

对父母的早期投资是否即使在儿童的处理效应似乎迅速消退时仍能带来回报？我们通过利用一项田野实验来回答这个问题，该实验将拥有3-5岁儿童的家庭随机分配到育儿项目、学前班或对照组，并结合十年后发生的一项自然实验（Covid-19学校关闭）。在学校关闭之前，所有组别对学业成绩的处理效应均已消退至零。然而，随着Covid-19学校关闭，育儿项目的效应重新显现：对照组和学前班组的测试成绩下降约0.25-0.30个标准差，而育儿组的成绩仅下降0.11个标准差（差异：p = 0.01）。我们的发现表明，育儿项目能够产生潜在人力资本，这种资本只有在互补的学校投入被移除时才会显现。这一结果与父母投资和学校投资在技能生产中作为替代品相一致。由于这种潜在的父母能力恰好在学校投入失效时发挥作用，针对早期儿童项目的标准测量工具可能会遗漏至关重要的状态依存价值。
