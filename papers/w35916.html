The Long March to Innovation: R&D and Competitive Evolution in the Indian Pharmaceutical Industry

通往创新的长征：印度制药业的研发与竞争演化

Chirantan Chatterjee, Aniruddh Bhaskaran, Matthew J. Higgins

健康与医疗 中国相关 印度、英国

Abstract

TRIPS (1995) has played a major role in shaping the Indian pharmaceutical industry. Conventional theory suggests that strong intellectual property rights (IPR) frameworks should strengthen innovation incentives. Yet, two decades after TRIPS, growth in India’s novel innovation pipeline has stalled. This chapter challenges the assumption that stronger IPR alone would catalyze discovery-based R&D. We utilize trial phase transition data from the Biomedtracker database, finding that the Indian pipeline faces a bottleneck in transitioning clinical trials specifically from Phase 1 to Phase 2, supported by a null result in transitions from Phase 1 to Phase 3, benchmarked against China and the United Kingdom. We argue that this reflects binding constraints in the Indian ecosystem that are likely orthogonal to IPR, accompanied by a within-firm shift toward non-novel programs. We corroborate these arguments through interviews with Indian pharmaceutical industry experts. Our findings should inform thinking about normative IPR in developing economies.

中文摘要

TRIPS（1995）在塑造印度制药业方面发挥了重要作用。传统理论认为，强有力的知识产权（IPR）框架应增强创新激励。然而，在TRIPS之后二十年，印度新药创新管线的增长已经停滞。本章挑战了仅靠更强IPR就能催化发现型研发的假设。我们利用Biomedtracker数据库的试验阶段转换数据，发现印度管线在临床试验转换中面临瓶颈，特别是在从1期到2期的转换中，并以从1期到3期转换的零结果作为支持，同时以中国和英国为基准。我们认为，这反映了印度生态系统中可能独立于IPR的约束性限制，并伴随企业向非新颖项目的内部转移。我们通过对印度制药行业专家的访谈佐证了这些论点。我们的发现应为发展中经济体规范性IPR的思考提供参考。
