Global Ripple Effects of Corporate Tax Reforms

公司税改革的全球涟漪效应

Sebastian Dyrda, Guangbin Hong, Muhammad Ali Sajid, Joseph B. Steinberg

公共财政与税收 美国

Abstract

We study the cross-country effects of corporate tax reforms in a fragmented global tax regime. Using firm-level evidence on the 2017 U.S. Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) and a quantitative general-equilibrium model, we illustrate how multinational enterprises (MNEs) propagate local policy shocks globally. Our framework emphasizes two properties of intangible capital: nonrivalry and mobile ownership. The TCJA generated positive outward ripple effects through two channels. First, it boosted U.S. MNEs’ intangible investment, raising output at their foreign subsidiaries. Second, it raised tangible investment by the U.S. subsidiaries of foreign MNEs, inducing their foreign parents to expand intangible investment at home. Conversely, a Global Minimum Tax (GMT) that allows low-tax jurisdictions to impose top-up taxes on U.S. MNEs’ affiliate earnings generates negative inward ripple effects on GDP and tax revenue for the United States. These findings illustrate that there is no such thing as a purely domestic corporate tax policy.

中文摘要

我们研究了碎片化全球税收制度下公司税改革的跨国效应。利用关于2017年美国《减税与就业法案》（TCJA）的企业层面证据以及一个定量一般均衡模型，我们说明了跨国企业（MNEs）如何将局部政策冲击在全球范围内传导。我们的框架强调了无形资本的两个特性：非竞争性和所有权流动性。TCJA通过两个渠道产生了正向的向外涟漪效应。首先，它促进了美国跨国企业的无形投资，提高了其外国子公司的产出。其次，它增加了外国跨国企业美国子公司的有形投资，诱导其外国母公司扩大在本国的无形投资。相反，允许低税收管辖区对美国跨国企业关联公司收益征收补足税的全球最低税（GMT），则会对美国的GDP和税收收入产生负向的向内涟漪效应。这些发现表明，不存在纯粹国内的公司税政策。
