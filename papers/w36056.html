Chips & Tokens at the Fault Lines: Two Stacks in One Fragmenting World

断层线上的芯片与代币：一个碎片化世界中的两个技术栈

Fabio Natalucci, Alessandro Rebucci

金融与资产定价 中国相关 美国

Abstract

The United States and China have each built the capacity to inflict serious economic damage on each other. This paper argues that the resulting mutual vulnerability now constrains both sides and prevents further economic decoupling. The paper maps this contest across two stacks: the real economy stack of computing, semiconductors and rare earths, and the financial stack of dollar dominance and digital finance. On the real side, an analysis of global earnings call reports, run through large language models, suggests that this mutual vulnerability was already shaping the global corporate narrative in 2025. On the financial side, the paper argues that digital finance could tip this balance either way. Portable U.S. dollar-denominated digital assets could deepen U.S. dollar dominance and erode China's capital controls, tilting the balance toward Washington and inviting more fragmentation. A drift of transactions onto offshore rails like Tether could do the opposite, eroding Washington’s enforcement power behind sanctions and, counterintuitively, restraining it. Both the U.S. and China, meanwhile, run internally inconsistent policies. The U.S. is closing its door to trade while keeping an open financial account, and China is doing the reverse. These configurations are ultimately unsustainable and could undermine the arsenals that sustain the equilibrium.

中文摘要

美国和中国都已建立起对彼此造成严重经济损害的能力。本文认为，由此产生的相互脆弱性现在约束着双方，并阻止进一步的经济脱钩。本文在两个技术栈上描绘这场竞争：计算、半导体和稀土构成的实体经济栈，以及美元主导地位和数字金融构成的金融栈。在实体方面，通过大型语言模型对全球财报电话会议报告的分析表明，这种相互脆弱性在2025年已经在塑造全球企业叙事。在金融方面，本文认为数字金融可能使这一平衡向任一方向倾斜。可携带的美元计价数字资产可能加深美元主导地位并侵蚀中国的资本管制，使平衡向华盛顿倾斜并招致更多碎片化。交易向Tether等离岸轨道漂移则可能产生相反效果，侵蚀华盛顿制裁背后的执法权力，并且反直觉地约束它。与此同时，美国和中国都运行着内部不一致的政策。美国在关闭贸易大门的同时保持金融账户开放，中国则相反。这些配置最终不可持续，并可能削弱维持均衡的武器库。
