Like a Good Neighbor: Childhood Neighbors Influence Occupation Choice

如好邻居：童年邻居影响职业选择

Michael J. Andrews, Ryan R. Hill, Joseph Price, Riley Wilson

教育与劳动 美国

Abstract

Occupation choice is a key determinant of economic mobility. This paper studies how childhood neighbors influence occupational choices and later-life earnings. Using linked historical U.S. census records for over six million boys and four million girls, we reconstruct neighborhood microgeography to identify childhood neighbors and their occupations. We find that living next door to someone in a given occupation increases the probability of working in that same occupation 30 years later by about 10 percent relative to other children on the same street. The transmission effect is higher with more intense exposure, in more connected neighborhoods, and when neighboring families are more similar. Exposure to high-income or highly educated neighbors has lasting economic effects, leading to significant gains in adult income and education even compared to other children from the same street. These findings suggest that neighborhood networks play an important role in shaping intergenerational economic mobility.

中文摘要

职业选择是经济流动性的关键决定因素。本文研究童年邻居如何影响职业选择和晚年收入。我们使用超过600万名男孩和400万名女孩的关联历史美国人口普查记录，重建社区微观地理，以识别童年邻居及其职业。我们发现，与同一条街上的其他孩子相比，住在从事某一职业的人隔壁，会使30年后从事同一职业的概率提高约10%。当接触强度更高、社区联系更紧密以及邻里家庭更相似时，这种传递效应更强。接触高收入或高教育程度的邻居会产生持久的经济影响，即使与同一条街上的其他孩子相比，也会带来成年后收入和教育的显著提升。这些发现表明，社区网络在塑造代际经济流动性方面发挥重要作用。
