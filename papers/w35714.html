Behavioral Signatures of Cognitive Noise

认知噪声的行为特征

Ryan Oprea, Michael Woodford

环境与气候 未指明区域

Abstract

The cognitive noise hypothesis is an increasingly popular explanation for a wide range of puzzling regularities in human behavior. Using simple, familiar tools and minimal assumptions drawn from neuroscience, cognitive noise models provide a parsimonious, unified explanation for a number of the most important anomalies documented in behavioral economics. The hypothesis proposes that limited cognitive resources force the brain to represent information imprecisely, generating coarser decisions, stochastic behavior and subjective uncertainty. Because brains deal with this kind of imprecision in a broadly Bayesian manner, imprecision leads to a number of well-known behavioral biases that may in fact be optimal given that imprecision. Because brains also allocate scarce cognitive resources adaptively, these biases and their severity are impacted by seemingly-irrelevant aspects of the choice environment, providing an explanation for a number of well-known context and experience effects that are otherwise difficult to explain. We review the basic structure of these models, their behavioral consequences, and the growing body of empirical evidence supporting them.

中文摘要

认知噪声假说越来越成为解释人类行为中各种令人费解的规律性的流行理论。利用简单、熟悉的工具和源自神经科学的最小假设，认知噪声模型为行为经济学中记录的最重要的一些异常现象提供了简洁、统一的解释。该假说提出，有限的认知资源迫使大脑不精确地表征信息，从而导致更粗糙的决策、随机行为和主观不确定性。由于大脑以大致贝叶斯的方式处理这种不精确性，不精确性会导致许多众所周知的、且在这种不精确性下可能实际上是最优的行为偏差。由于大脑也会适应性地分配稀缺的认知资源，这些偏差及其严重程度会受到选择环境中看似无关的方面的影响，这为许多众所周知的、否则难以解释的背景效应和经验效应提供了解释。我们回顾了这些模型的基本结构、它们的行为后果，以及支持它们的越来越多的经验证据。
