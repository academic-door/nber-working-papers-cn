NBER Working Paper w35933所在周报

Losing Their Religion

失去他们的宗教

Richard Murphy, Shqiponja Telhaj

教育与劳动 阿尔巴尼亚

Abstract

This paper studies whether governments can change social norms that constrain women’s education and work, and through which mechanism. Albania’s 1967 religion ban, which created the world’s first atheist state, provides an extreme natural experiment: religious practice was outlawed nationwide, but a 1948 classification of historic monuments determined which religious buildings remained standing. We find larger increases in female employment and schooling where no monument survived. The public visibility of the old religious order appears to be the mechanism: effects are largest where no monument remained, smaller with rural-only monuments, and smallest with urban monuments; they also decline as monument count rises, regardless of denomination. We establish how social norms can outlive formal authority.

中文摘要

本文研究政府能否改变约束女性教育和工作的社会规范，以及通过何种机制。阿尔巴尼亚1967年的宗教禁令创造了世界上第一个无神论国家，提供了一个极端的自然实验：宗教活动在全国范围内被取缔，但1948年对历史遗迹的分类决定了哪些宗教建筑得以保留。我们发现，在没有遗迹留存的地方，女性就业和 schooling 的增长更大。旧宗教秩序的公共可见性似乎是机制所在：在没有遗迹留存的地方效应最大，仅有农村遗迹时效应较小，有城市遗迹时效应最小；无论教派如何，效应也随着遗迹数量的增加而下降。我们确立了社会规范如何能够比正式权威更长久地存在。
